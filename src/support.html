Palette Studio for Miniatures

Palette Studio is a free tool for miniature painters. Pick a paint or a colour, and it builds a colour scheme matched to real paints you can buy. A question or a problem? See Contact and privacy.

Start a scheme

What do Roll a scheme and ↻ (Pick a colour for me) do?

Both give you a random idea. You can undo each one with ↶ Undo (Ctrl/⌘ Z).

  • Roll a scheme (the die at the start of the row of harmonies; where that row is narrow, for example on a phone, the button shows only “Roll”) picks a new harmony at random for the starting colour. The starting paint stays the same, in the same role (Main or Accent). The lightness, the colours you locked or added, and the pop accent do not change. It never picks Custom or the harmony you have now. For a neutral colour (a black, white or grey), it picks only from the schemes for neutrals: Neutral + pop, Duotone, Warm / cool and Shades.
  • ↻ Pick a colour for me (under the lightness slider; beside it on a phone) gives the scheme a new random colour: a random hue and strength. For most colours, it keeps the lightness and the harmony. On black, on white, and on colours near them, every hue looks grey. So there, ↻ moves the lightness by the smallest step that lets the new colour show, and a message tells you the new lightness. On a black, white or grey colour, the scheme is one for neutrals. The new colour has a hue, so the scheme changes back to a harmony with a hue: the one you had before, or Complementary.

How do I keep a colour or a paint I already chose?

On the Recipe tab, the Secondary, Accent and Metal cards each have two controls:

  • Lock colour keeps the role's target colour. When you change the harmony, roll a scheme or press ↻, the other roles change and this one stays.
  • 📌 Choose pot… lets you pick the paint for the role, for example a gold you already used on the model. The tone ladder then starts from that pot. The card still shows how far the pot is from the target colour (ΔE), so you can see if it fits the scheme.

On the live palette, the pencil on a column opens the same list of paints. Its last row lets you set a custom colour. On the wheel, drag a role's node to lock that role at a new colour.

A line under the live palette lists what you hold. Select Release to let a role change again. Holds stay when you pick a new starting paint, and a shared link keeps them. The Primary role always comes from the starting paint, so you cannot hold it.

Matching and ΔE

How the matching works

For each colour in your scheme, Palette Studio finds the nearest real paint. It measures how different two colours look with ΔE 2000 (say “delta E”), a standard measure of colour difference. A lower number is a closer match.

Every match shows its ΔE and a label:

  • Indistinguishable: ΔE 1 or less. You cannot see a difference.
  • Excellent: ΔE 2 or less.
  • Good: ΔE 3.5 or less.
  • Fair: ΔE 5 or less. You can see the difference side by side.
  • Loose: ΔE 10 or less.
  • Poor: more than ΔE 10. It is a different colour.

The colour values for real paints are approximate. So a match is never exact, and the ΔE tells you how close it is.

What is "the maker's tones" on a Recipe card?

Some makers sell a colour as a set of tones. Warhammer Tone Pro has five tones of each colour, and Reaper sells most Master Series colours as a triad: a shadow, a midtone and a highlight. When the pot of a role is one of those paints, its card has one more row with the whole set, darkest first, and your pot is marked. The row shows the set as the maker made it. It has no ΔE, because these are the maker's steps and not matches. Select a tone to see its details.

The wash in a Recipe

In the Wash · base · highlight ladder, the wash step looks for a real wash, shade or ink. It has three tries:

  • First, the wash that is nearest by ΔE (ΔE 10 or less).
  • If there is none, the wash that is nearest by its colour. The step then shows a gap in degrees (8° or less). The gap compares the colour of the wash, not how dark it looks. It does not tell you how strong the wash is, so compare the pots. See What if the store does not have a paint? for the gap.
  • If there is still none, the step shows a base paint with watered down: thin that paint and use it as the wash.

Use my paints

Set it in ⋯ → Use my paints. It changes the matches in the live palette, the Recipe, the shopping list and the Accessibility tab. The Other brands tab always lists every paint.

  • Off: every paint can match.
  • Prefer: a paint you own counts as about ΔE 6 closer than it is. So it wins a close call, but not a clear one. The ΔE you see is the true value.
  • Only: only paints you own can match, even when the match is poor.

The Metal part of a scheme has its own rule. The app wants a gold, a silver or a gunmetal there, and with Prefer it uses a metallic that you own unless that pot is far from the one it wants. The pot then says A metallic you own in place of a word such as Loose or Poor. Its ΔE is still the true value: it is the distance to the app's own gold, silver or gunmetal, not a mark for your pot.

While Prefer or Only is on, a note under the live palette says so. To use this setting, first mark the paints you own on the Shelf. When you then go back to the Studio, the app asks one time: Prefer my paints, Only my paints or Not now. You can change your answer in ⋯ at any time.

Brand: keep a scheme in one brand

Set it in ⋯ → Brand. Choose the brand in the list. The list starts on the brand that you own the most paints of. It changes the same matches as Use my paints, and the Other brands tab always lists every paint.

  • Any: every brand can match.
  • Prefer: a paint of your brand counts as ΔE 2 closer than it is. So it wins a close call, but not a clear one. The ΔE you see is the true value.
  • Only: only paints of your brand can match, even when the match is poor.

You can use Brand and Use my paints together. A paint that you own still comes first: it counts as about ΔE 6 closer, and a paint of your brand as ΔE 2 closer. While Prefer or Only is on, a note under the live palette says so.

Why doesn't a match look exactly like my paint?

The colour values for real paints are approximate. Every match shows its ΔE score and a plain-language label, so you can see how close it is (see How the matching works). The paint data comes from the community dataset Arcturus5404/miniature-paints.

How do I see everything about one paint?

Open its Paint details. There are three ways:

  • In the Recipe or in Other brands, select the paint's name. In a tone ladder, select a step.
  • On the Shelf, open the paint's menu (a right-click, or a long press on a touch screen) and choose Paint details.
  • In the list under the seed field, open the paint's menu in the same way.

The sheet shows the paint and its finish, and it lets you mark the paint as owned or put it on your shopping list. Under that are up to three lists, each with a ΔE:

  • Almost the same colour: paints that are ΔE 1 or less from this one in the paint data, with the same finish. The colours in the app are approximate, so compare the pots before you use one in place of the other.
  • Closest in other brands: the five nearest paints from other makers.
  • Closest on your shelf: the three nearest paints that you own. If one is not an exact match, a line tells you how to change it, for example “To match: darken slightly”.

Select a row to see that paint's details. Start a scheme from this takes the paint to the Studio.

What is the small icon on some paints?

A swatch shows the paint's own colour and nothing more. A colour cannot show that a paint is a metallic or a wash, so a paint that is not a flat colour has one small icon in the bottom-right corner of its swatch:

  • A gem: a metallic.
  • A drop: a wash, a shade, an ink or a glaze.
  • A drop with its lower half filled: a Contrast or speed paint.
  • A circle with a small square: a gloss effect.
  • Drips: a slime effect. Dots: a texture. A star: any other effect paint.
  • A small sun: a fluorescent or neon paint.

An ordinary base paint, layer paint or Dry paint has no icon. The same icon is in the tag beside the paint's name. On a very small swatch there is no icon, and the tag or the name tells you the finish.

A paint is missing, or its colour is wrong

Tell me through the contact page. Include the brand, the paint name, and what looks wrong.

Your shelf

What is the quickest way to mark the paints I own?

Open the Shelf and press List. With no search, the list is an index: one line for each range of each brand, such as Warhammer Colour · Base. Open the lines that you own, and tick each paint that you have. A line shows how many of its paints you own. To go to one brand, type its name in the search: “citadel” finds Warhammer Colour.

How do I mark my paints on a phone?

In the grid, tap a paint to mark it: owned, then on the shopping list, then not marked. When you search, each paint in the grid shows its name and brand. To read names without marking, hold a finger on a paint for a moment, then slide it across the shelf. A label above your finger names each paint. Lift your finger to stop: this marks nothing. Hold still for longer to open the menu.

To mark many paints at once, press Select. Tap paints, or hold one and drag: every paint from that one to your finger is selected, row by row. The actions come up at the bottom: Mark owned, Add to shopping list or Clear.

To show only some kinds of paint, open Filters and turn on one or more types, such as Layer and Wash. When a search or a filter hides paints, the line under the title says how many show, with Clear filters.

Which colours and tones am I missing?

Open the Shelf and press Map (the wheel icon beside Grid and List). The small wheel shows each paint you own as a dot in its colour, and each paint on your shopping list as a blue ring. A hatched part of the wheel is a colour family with no paint you own. Tap it to go to that colour in the grid.

The grid below shows tones: colour across and lightness down, from Highlight to Shade (on a phone, colour runs down). A dashed square is a gap, for example no dark green to shade a green with. Tap a square to see its paints. For a gap, or a tone with one paint, you also see the three closest pots, each with how close it is (ΔE). Add puts a pot on your shopping list. A paint on the shopping list does not fill a gap until you own it. The search and the filters change what the map shows.

Where is my shelf saved?

Only in this browser, on this device. Palette Studio has no accounts and no server, so there is no other copy. Clearing the site's data or using a private window removes it. Safari can also clear it when you do not open the site for some time. To keep your shelf safe, add the app to your Home Screen, or back it up now and then (⋯ → Back up everything).

How do I delete my shelf?

Open ⋯ → About and choose Delete my shelf and settings…. It asks first, and it lets you save a backup. Then it removes your owned marks, your shopping list, your saved schemes and your settings from this device. You cannot undo this.

Schemes

How do I keep a scheme?

Press Save on the line above the starting paint, give the scheme a name, and press Save again. It goes to Schemes, next to Studio and Shelf at the top. When you change a saved scheme, the line says Changed: Save keeps the change in it, and Save as new keeps both. Nothing is saved until you press Save. In Schemes you can open, rename, share or delete a scheme. Deleting a scheme keeps its paints on your shopping list.

Shopping and the store

How do I put a scheme's paints on my shopping list?

On the Recipe tab, + Add … pots to the shopping list adds the pot of each part of the scheme (Primary, Secondary, Accent, Metal) that you do not own: four pots at most. Add … ladder pots, beside it, adds the washes, shadows and highlights of the tone ladders that show. The flat paints of the NMM recipe are a different way to paint the metal part, so they have their own action on the Metal card: Add the … NMM pots. You can also add one paint at a time: Add under a pot, or select a ladder step and choose Shopping list.

How do I use my shopping list in a store?

Open the Shelf, press List, then Shopping list. The paints show in the order a store racks them: brand, then range. By project groups them by the scheme they are for. Tick a paint when it is in your basket: it is then owned. Tap it again to undo. Add a count sets how many pots you want. Print puts the list on paper.

What if the store does not have a paint?

In the Shelf's list, press Not on the rack? on the paint (on a phone, the arrows button beside its count). You see the three closest pots, each with how close it is (ΔE) and a word for that (Good, Fair…). A wash only gets paints that flow as a wash does (washes and shades first, then Contrast paints and inks), a metallic only gets metallics, and an effect paint only gets the same effect. A pot you own already comes first when it is a Good match or better, because you then have nothing to buy.

Washes and shades are compared in another way. Some makers show a wash as a thin coat on white, and others show the liquid in the pot, so the same wash can be light in one brand and almost black in another. The app thus compares the colour of the wash and not how dark it looks. You see a gap in degrees in place of ΔE: 4° or less is a Close colour, 8° or less is a Near colour, and more is an Other colour. The gap does not tell you how strong the wash is, so compare the pots in the store.

For inks and Contrast paints the colours in the app are rough too, so their swaps never say more than Good.

Press Use: the new pot goes on your list for the first paint, under the heading of its own brand, so you find it when you are at that rack. If the paint was a pot in a scheme, the scheme uses the new pot too. The Recipe then says “Swapped in the store for …”, with Use the original to go back.

If the new pot is not on the rack either, press Not on the rack? on it. You then see the next closest pots to the paint you first wanted, not to the swap.

If the store does not carry a brand at all, switch it off under This store carries. The row has every brand in the app. Every paint of a brand that is off then shows its closest pots from the other brands, and no swap comes from a brand that is off (a pot you own still counts: it is on your shelf). So switch off each brand the store does not have, also a brand that is not on your list.

Some paints have a No longer made tag: the maker stopped making them. You can still own one, so it stays in the app. On your shopping list, such a paint shows its closest pots at once, because no store has it. The Recipe does not suggest a paint that is no longer made, unless you own it or it is your starting paint.

How do I get my list from my computer to my phone?

On the computer, open ⋯ → Take to the store… (or Take to the store… in the Shelf's list). Choose all your paints or one project. Scan the QR code with your phone's camera, or send the link to your phone. On the phone, the app asks before it adds the paints to the shopping list there. Paints you own on the phone stay owned. Nothing goes to a server: the list is in the link.

In the store, tick each paint as you buy it. Then press Send “bought”… above the list, and open that link on the computer. The app asks, then marks those paints owned there too. It only adds owned marks, and opening the link again changes nothing.

The first time a project without a name leaves the app, it asks for a name once. Skip keeps the automatic name (for example “Death Guard Green · triadic”). The name is part of the link you send.

Save as text in the same panel gives a plain file of the list: each paint with a [ ] box, in rack order, with no colour codes. ⋯ → Save the recipe saves the scheme on screen.

Backup and other devices

How do I move my shelf to another device?

⋯ → Back up everything here, then ⋯ → Restore… on the other device. It replaces the shelf there. To add paints from a paintRack list without replacing anything, use Import… on the Shelf.

A backup holds your owned marks, your shopping list, your saved schemes and your settings: theme, Use my paints, the tone ladder and Include Contrast. Before Restore… replaces anything, it shows what is on this device and what is in the backup, and it lets you save a backup of this shelf first.

Does it work offline?

Yes. After your first visit, the website works without a connection, and you can add it to your home screen. The iPhone and iPad app works offline from the start.

Keyboard shortcuts

Ctrl/⌘ Z
Studio: undo a palette change. Ctrl/⌘ Shift Z or Ctrl/⌘ Y redoes it.
← → ↑ ↓
Wheel: hue (← →) and saturation, how vivid (↑ ↓). Hold Shift for bigger steps. Lightness slider: the arrow keys.
[ ]
Wheel: go to the previous or next colour.
+ −
Wheel: add a colour, or remove the last colour you added.
← → ↑ ↓
Shelf: move between paints. Hold Shift to select more.
← → ↑ ↓ Enter
Shelf map: move between tones. Enter shows the paints in a tone, or the pots that fill it.
↓
Paint field (“Paint or #hex”): open the list. In the colour grid, the arrow keys move in all four directions. Home and End go to the start and the end of a row; with Ctrl, to the first and the last paint. Tab goes to Sort. In a list of names, ↑ and ↓ move.
Enter
Paint field: start the scheme from the paint or the colour that the list shows as active. A hex such as #9A1115 changes the starting colour as you type; 9A1115 or #9A1 needs Enter. Other brands tab: show the matches for the palette colour that has focus.
Shift F10
Open the mark menu: on the Shelf for the selection (Start a scheme from this, Mark owned, Add to shopping list and Clear mark), and in the paint field's list for the active paint (Mark owned, Add to shopping list and Clear mark). The Menu key on a Windows keyboard does the same. On a touch screen, press and hold a paint; with a mouse, right-click it.
P U X
Shelf: mark the selection as owned, add it to the shopping list, or clear its marks.
Esc
Close a list, menu, sheet or dialog. In the paint field, after a hex that changed the starting colour as you typed, Esc also puts back the starting paint from before. On the Shelf, clear the selection.

Contact and privacy

Use the contact page at ryanmette.com. To help me fix a problem quickly, include:

  • what you were doing when it went wrong;
  • your device and browser, or your iPhone or iPad model and iOS version;
  • if the problem is with a scheme, a link to it (⋯ → Share link).

The app can write these details for you. Nothing is sent: you copy them, and you choose to paste them into your message.
