Help and support

Palette Studio for Miniatures

Palette Studio is a free tool for miniature painters. Pick a paint or a colour, and it builds a colour scheme matched to real paints you can buy.

Get help

Use the contact page at ryanmette.com. To help me fix a problem quickly, include:

  • what you were doing when it went wrong;
  • your device and browser, or your iPhone or iPad model and iOS version;
  • if the problem is with a scheme, a link to it (⋯ → Share link).

How the matching works

For each colour in your scheme, Palette Studio finds the nearest real paint. It measures how different two colours look with ΔE 2000 (say “delta E”), a standard measure of colour difference. A lower number is a closer match.

Every match shows its ΔE and a label:

  • Indistinguishable: ΔE 1 or less. You cannot see a difference.
  • Excellent: ΔE 2 or less.
  • Good: ΔE 3.5 or less.
  • Fair: ΔE 5 or less. You can see the difference side by side.
  • Loose: ΔE 10 or less.
  • Poor: more than ΔE 10. It is a different colour.

The colour values for real paints are approximate. So a match is never exact, and the ΔE tells you how close it is.

Use my paints

Set it in ⋯ → Use my paints. It changes the matches in the live palette, the Recipe, the shopping list and the Accessibility tab. The Other brands tab always lists every paint.

  • Off: every paint can match.
  • Prefer: a paint you own counts as about ΔE 6 closer than it is. So it wins a close call, but not a clear one. The ΔE you see is the true value.
  • Only: only paints you own can match, even when the match is poor.

While Prefer or Only is on, a note under the live palette says so. To use this setting, first mark the paints you own on the Shelf.

Wheel: add a colour, or remove the last colour you added.

Shelf: move between paints. Hold Shift to select more.

Paint field (“Paint or #hex”): open the list. In the colour grid, the arrow keys move in all four directions. Home and End go to the start and the end of a row; with Ctrl, to the first and the last paint. Tab goes to Sort. In a list of names, ↑ and ↓ move.
Enter
Paint field: start the scheme from the paint or the colour that the list shows as active. A hex such as #9A1115 changes your colour as you type; 9A1115 or #9A1 needs Enter. Other brands tab: show the matches for the palette colour that has focus.
Shift F10
Open the mark menu: on the Shelf for the selection (Make this your paint, Mark owned, Add to shopping list and Clear mark), and in the paint field's list for the active paint (Mark owned, Add to shopping list and Clear mark). The Menu key on a Windows keyboard does the same. On a touch screen, press and hold a paint; with a mouse, right-click it.
P U X
Shelf: mark the selection as owned, add it to the shopping list, or clear its marks.
Esc
Close a list, menu, sheet or dialog. In the paint field, after a hex that changed your colour as you typed, Esc also puts back your paint from before. On the Shelf, clear the selection.

Common questions

What do Roll a scheme and ↻ (Pick a colour for me) do?

Both give you a random idea. You can undo each one with ↶ Undo (Ctrl/⌘ Z).

  • Roll a scheme (the die at the start of the row of harmonies; where that row is narrow, for example on a phone, the button shows only “Roll”) picks a new harmony at random for your colour. Your paint stays the same, in the same role (Main or Accent). The lightness, the colours you locked or added, and the pop accent do not change. It never picks Custom or the harmony you have now. For a neutral colour (a black, white or grey), it picks only from the schemes for neutrals: Neutral + pop, Duotone, Warm / cool and Shades.
  • ↻ Pick a colour for me (under the lightness slider; beside it on a phone) gives the scheme a new random colour: a random hue and strength. For most colours, it keeps the lightness and the harmony. On black, on white, and on colours near them, every hue looks grey. So there, ↻ moves the lightness by the smallest step that lets the new colour show, and a message tells you the new lightness. On a black, white or grey colour, the scheme is one for neutrals. The new colour has a hue, so the scheme changes back to a harmony with a hue: the one you had before, or Complementary.

Why doesn't a match look exactly like my paint?

The colour values for real paints are approximate. Every match shows its ΔE score and a plain-language label, so you can see how close it is (see How the matching works). The paint data comes from the community dataset Arcturus5404/miniature-paints.

A paint is missing, or its colour is wrong

Tell me through the contact page. Include the brand, the paint name, and what looks wrong.

Where is my shelf saved?

Only in this browser, on this device. Palette Studio has no accounts and no server, so there is no other copy. Clearing the site's data or using a private window removes it. Safari can also clear it when you do not open the site for some time. To keep your shelf safe, add the app to your Home Screen, or back it up now and then (⋯ → Back up everything).

How do I move my shelf to another device?

⋯ → Back up everything here, then ⋯ → Restore… on the other device. It replaces the shelf there. To add paints from a paintRack list without replacing anything, use Import… on the Shelf.

A backup holds your owned marks, your shopping list and your settings: theme, language, Use my paints, the tone ladder and Include Contrast. Before Restore… replaces anything, it shows what is on this device and what is in the backup, and it lets you save a backup of this shelf first.

How do I delete my shelf?

Open ⋯ → About & data and choose Delete my shelf and settings…. It asks first, and it lets you save a backup. Then it removes your owned marks, your shopping list and your settings from this device. You cannot undo this.

Does it work offline?

Yes. After your first visit, the website works without a connection, and you can add it to your home screen. The iPhone and iPad app works offline from the start.
