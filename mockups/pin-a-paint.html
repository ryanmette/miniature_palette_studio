Pin a paint: keep the paints you chose, and get the rest

Mockup, 2026-10-28 · app 1.9.0 · engine and dataset from git 8ff5a59 (dataset 1.6.0, 3,225 paints)

Page theme

The request (Ryan, 2026-10-28): “If I am a Death Guard player and I know I’m using Death Guard Green, and I also want to add Balthasar Gold, what other paints are recommended? Is there a path for a user to do that?” Then: “Yes, mock up pin a paint.”

Short answer today: no. You can seed the scheme with Death Guard Green. But the app chooses the metal for you, from the hue of your seed. For Death Guard Green it chooses gunmetal, and it gives you Vallejo Dark Steel in every harmony. No control lets you say “my metal is Balthasar Gold”. See today’s numbers.

The use cases. This is UC-3, “Complete a scheme I started” in docs/USE_CASES.md: “I have a body and an accent — what else?” Lock both seeds, then get the secondary, the metal, the washes and the highlights. It was never built. It is also UC-10, “Lock a paint, spin the rest”: keep one paint, and let the rest of the scheme move around it.

What “pin” means on this page. A pin tells the app: “this role uses this paint”. The role can be Primary, Secondary, Accent or Metal. The app keeps the pinned paint in that role. It does not replace it with a nearer match. It builds the other roles around the pins. It also gives every role a wash and a highlight, the pinned roles too. For Balthasar Gold, the wash and the highlight must suit bronze, not steel.

A pin is not today’s lock. Today a lock (🔓 on a live-palette column) keeps a colour (a point on the wheel) when you press ↻ or change the harmony. The app then finds the nearest paint to that colour again, so the paint can change. The Metal column has no lock. A pin keeps a paint, and a pin can go on Metal.

Every direction must also work with: the ΔE label on every match (§2: never say a match is exact) · Use my paints in ⋯ (off · prefer · only) · share links (the pins go in the URL) · undo and redo · accent-seed mode · neutral mode · a 390px phone.

How to read the numbers. This page runs the committed engine (src/js from git 8ff5a59) on the full dataset, in your browser. A plain number is what the app shows today. A number next to ◇ Projection uses the engine’s own functions for something the app does not do yet, for example a pinned metal. In a frame, a dashed grey outline marks new app UI. It is a mark on this page, not part of the app.

Why the metal is gunmetal

The app does not search for a metal that suits your paints. It reads the hue of the seed and chooses one of three fixed metal colours. Death Guard Green has a hue of 74.3° and a Lab chroma of 24.6 (so it is not neutral). That hue is in the gap between the gold band (under 70°) and the silver band (150°–280°), so it gets gunmetal. It misses gold by 4.3°. Gold would not help: Balthasar Gold is a bronze (hue 22.9°, lightness 49%), and it is far from all three ideals.

What each direction must answer

  1. Where do you pin: on a Plan role card, on a live-palette column, in the seed toolbar, or somewhere else?
  2. How do you choose the paint? Reuse the seed field’s search: one field for a paint name, brand words or a hex, and a list that only picks (mockups/paints-picker.html#recommended).
  3. What does a pinned role look like? A pin is a state, so it is a badge or a fill, not a ring (§3.5). It must not move the layout when you pin or unpin (§3.4).
  4. The other roles. Pinned paints are taken first, so no other role reuses them. Does a pinned colour (not a metal) change which harmony fits? Today no harmony puts Balthasar Gold near Secondary or Accent: the nearest is Analogous Accent, ΔE 16.5.
  5. Ladders for a pinned role. A wash and a highlight for every role, the pinned roles too, with the NMM recipe for Metal.
  6. Honesty (§2). Never call a pinned paint a “match”. Show how far it is from what the harmony suggested.
  7. Use my paints = Only, and you pin a paint you do not own: does the pin win, and what does the matching note say?
  8. Share link. Which URL parameter carries the pins? It must be stable, and an old link must still open.
  9. Undo and redo. Is a pin or an unpin one step?
  10. Accent-seed mode (the pick is the Accent) and neutral mode (a grey seed holds Primary): what can you pin?
  11. A 390px phone, with touch targets of 44px.

A · Pin a paint on the role card Recommended

Each role card in the Plan gets a 📌 Pin a paint action. It opens the seed field’s search, for that role only (Metal shows metals first). The paint you choose becomes the role’s paint, and the role’s ladders are built from it. The other roles still come from the harmony. On the live palette, a pinned role’s column shows a 📌 pin chip in its dock row, and an empty dock row gets a 📌 pin button. Unpin gives the role back to the harmony.

The flow: Death Guard Green, then pin Balthasar Gold on Metal

Before the pin, every number is what the committed app shows today. After the pin, the Metal numbers are a projection ◇ Projection: the app cannot pin yet. The page makes them with the engine’s own ladder steps (§7) and nearestPaint(), and the pinned paint’s hex as the Metal ideal.

Why grey, not red, after the pin. In the list, the §3.2 label grades each paint against the suggestion, so you can compare before you choose. After you pin, the pinned paint is not a match that the app found. Its ΔE to itself is 0, and that is not a quality. So the card keeps the number, the distance from the suggestion, with a plain word (on harmony ≤ 3.5 · near harmony ≤ 10 · off harmony > 10) and a grey dot. The word is --text and the dot is --text-muted, so no tier colour reads as a match quality. --danger stays for failing or destructive things (§3.5), and your own choice is neither. Each rung of a pinned role shows its own ΔE, so a far rung is visible too.

What a pin does (the rules)

  1. A pin holds a paint, not a colour. Today’s lock (🔓) keeps a point on the wheel, and the nearest paint can change. A pin keeps the paint. It stays when you change the harmony, press ↻, move the seed with ⚓, or change Use my paints.
  2. Where. On all four Plan cards, in every harmony. On the live palette, an empty dock row gets 📌 pin. The dock row of the column that can take the seed keeps ⚓ seed here, so you pin that role from its card. Secondary has no live-palette column in Complementary, so you pin it from its card. Then a display-only Secondary column appears, as Metal has today, so the live palette still shows every role.
  3. The seed’s role has no pin. The seed is already your paint (⚓). To change it, change the seed. ⚓ seed here does not show on a pinned column: unpin it first.
  4. The list. For Metal, metallics come first, then other paints (for a flat or NMM metal). Each row shows its ΔE to the suggestion, with the §3.2 label. Your seed and paints pinned to other roles stay in the list, but you cannot pick them, and the row says why.
  5. The other roles. Pinned paints are taken first, so no other role reuses them. The harmony does not move to fit a pin. A pinned colour (not a metal) shows on the wheel as a 📌 marker at its hue; the role’s node stays at the suggestion.
  6. Ladders. A pinned role’s base rung is the pinned paint. The other rungs use the same steps (§7) from its hex: the wash (real wash media first, else “watered down”), the highlight, the tone ladder, and NMM for Metal. ◇ Projection
  7. Use my paints. A pin always holds, under Only too. A pinned paint that you do not own keeps “+ buy” and goes on the shopping list. Under Only the card says “not in your paints”. The rungs follow the setting, as they do today.
  8. Neutral mode. A grey seed holds Primary. In Neutral + pop and Duotone, a pin on Accent also sets the pop, so the bridge follows the pinned paint. In Warm / cool, a pin only replaces the role.
  9. Share link. One new parameter, pin: role.paint-id.HEX, with _ between pins. The app writes its query with URLSearchParams, which percent-encodes : and , but leaves . and _ alone, and no paint id contains either. The hex is the fallback, as c is for the seed. An old link has no pin, so it opens as today. If a paint id is not in the dataset, the nearest paint to the hex takes the role, with the “pick replaced” note. Today’s build ignores pin. For a grey seed with a pinned Accent, it still gets the pop from pp.
  10. Undo. A pin, an unpin and a change are one step each (↶ ↷, Ctrl+Z, ⇧Ctrl+Z).
  11. The colour link (§3.5). A pinned role’s card and its live-palette column carry the pin’s hex in data-hex, so hover or focus on one rings the other. The suggestion is the card’s left swatch only.

Pros / cons

Accessibility

  • Names and roles. “📌 Pin a paint…”, “Change…”, the 📌 dock and the pin chip are buttons with aria-haspopup="dialog" and a name that says the role (“Metal: pinned Citadel Balthasar Gold. Change or unpin.”). The sheet is role="dialog", labelled by its title. The field is a combobox with a listbox of options in labelled groups, as in the seed field. A row you cannot pick has aria-disabled="true" and says why.
  • Keyboard. Tab to the action, then Enter. Focus goes to the field. Type, then ↓ ↑ move the list cursor (aria-activedescendant), Enter pins, Esc closes. Focus comes back to the same place, which now reads “Change…”; “Unpin” is next to it. On a phone, Tab stays in the sheet. Ctrl+Z and ⇧Ctrl+Z undo and redo a pin, as ↶ ↷ do.
  • Screen readers. A pin or an unpin is announced in the frame’s live region, with the paint and its distance from the suggestion. The sheet has its own live region ([data-say]), and the result count is read after a 500ms pause. The toast is aria-hidden.
  • Focus and state. Every new control has the --focus ring. The list cursor is the .act ring (interaction). The list’s scroll box is not a Tab stop (tabindex="-1"): the field is the one stop, as in the seed field. In the desktop popover, Tab from its last control goes on to the control after the one that opened it, and ⇧Tab from its first control goes back to that control, so the Plan cards stay in the Tab order. The pin is a fill: --accent on --accent-weak, the seed badge’s pair (state, §3.5).
  • 44px on touch. In the phone frame, the card action row, the 📌 dock, the pin chip, the field, the rows and ✕ are var(--tap) tall.
  • Contrast. The new words on a live-palette column (“📌 pin”, “Off harmony”) are --text on --surface-2 (13.98:1 light, 12.83:1 dark). --text-muted there is only 4.20:1 in light.
  • Layout (§3.4). The action row has the same height in all three states, so Change… and Unpin sit where 📌 Pin a paint… was. “📌 Your paint” replaces “Nearest real paint” on the same line, the distance line has the shape of the ΔE line, and the left swatch never changes. The paint block shows a different paint, so its height can change with the length of the name and the brand line: at 390px, Balthasar Gold’s block is 33px shorter than Vallejo Dark Steel’s. The rungs under it then move by that amount, in one direction for each change. The app’s card does the same today when a new harmony picks a new paint. To stop it, the app must give the block a fixed number of lines.
  • Reduced motion. The sheet’s 200ms rise and the toast fade run only with prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference.
  • Forced colours. The pin pill and the pin chip get a CanvasText border, so the state stays visible without its fill.

B · “Paints I’m using”: you list your paints, the app finds their roles

You do not have to know the four roles. You add the paints you already chose to one strip, Paints I’m using, in any order. The app puts each paint in a role. It looks at the paint’s type first and its colour second: a metallic goes to Metal, a wash goes on a wash rung, an effect paint goes to Extras, and any other paint goes to the nearest free colour role. Then the harmony fills the free roles, and every role gets a wash and a highlight, also the roles that hold your paints. To put a paint in a different role, open its chip.

Share link, undo and the modes

  • Share link: a new parameter u (“using”). It lists each paint with the place it went: a role (P S A M), a rung (PW is the wash rung of Primary, PH its highlight rung) or Extras (X). The link stores the place, not only the paint, so a later change to the placement rules cannot move a shared plan. Paint ids are stable (§5.1). Each item is place.paint-id, with _ between items: the app writes its query with URLSearchParams, which leaves . and _ alone (it percent-encodes : and ,), and no paint id contains either. The worked example:
    https://palette.ryanmette.com/?p=citadel-base-death-guard-green&c=6D774D&h=complementary&u=M.citadel-base-balthasar-gold_S.citadel-layer-ushabti-bone_X.citadel-technical-nurgles-rot_PW.citadel-shade-agrax-earthshade
  • Old links still open. A link without u opens as today. The app skips a paint id that is not in the dataset, and it says “1 paint in this link is not in the dataset”. The seed stays in p and c, as today.
  • Undo and redo. Each add, move, remove and harmony switch is one step of the live palette’s ↶ ↷. A placement and its “Switch” offer are two steps, so ↶ after a switch puts back the old harmony and the old place.
  • Use my paints (⋯). A paint you added always holds its place, also with Only and when you do not own it: you chose it. Its chip says “not owned”, and the Plan offers + buy. Prefer and Only change only the free roles and the rungs. Equivalents still lists every paint.
  • Paints I’m using is not the Shelf. Adding a paint does not mark it as owned. The Plan counts it as to buy until you mark it.
  • Accent-seed mode. The seed holds Accent (§7: verbatim). A colour you add goes to Primary or Secondary. Example: seed Mephiston Red as the accent, add Death Guard Green: it goes to Primary (ΔE 25.2 from the Primary ideal #099396; Secondary is ΔE 46.4) ◇ Projection.
  • Neutral mode. A neutral seed holds Primary. The first colour you add goes to Accent and becomes the pop (the pp parameter follows it), so its drift is ΔE 0.0 ◇ Projection. Example: Citadel Mechanicus Standard Grey with Mephiston Red: the pop moves from the default #9C1626 to #960C09 (ΔE 7.0). In neutral mode the app makes no harmony offer.
  • The wheel. A colour you added shows as a square node. The harmony’s own point stays on the wheel, so you can see the gap. Metal and the rungs have no node, as today.
  • The live palette. A role that holds your paint shows it, with a Yours badge and its distance from the harmony’s ideal (Direction A’s words: on, near or off harmony, with the ΔE), so the one colour bar shows the gap too (§2). Complementary has no Secondary node, so a paint in Secondary gets a display-only column, like Metal today.

Pros / cons

Pros

  • It answers Ryan’s question in his words. “I use Death Guard Green and Balthasar Gold” becomes two chips. The painter does not need to know that the gold is “the Metal role”.
  • One place to add, any order. The search is the seed field’s pattern, and the list shows where each paint will go before you pick it. No surprise.
  • It covers more than roles. A wash goes on a wash rung, a bone can be a highlight, and an effect paint goes to Extras. A painter’s real list has these paints.
  • Honest. Each placed paint shows how far it is from what the harmony suggested, and the harmony’s own pick stays visible. The pin never shows as a ΔE 0 “match”.
  • Stable links. The link stores where each paint went, so a later change to the rules cannot move a shared plan.

Cons

  • The placement can be wrong. A painter’s paints seldom fit a harmony. Ushabti Bone is ΔE 22.1 from Secondary and ΔE 48.7 from Accent, so “nearest” is only a weak guess. The painter must check the chip and maybe move it.
  • One more thing on the studio column. The strip is about 175px tall on a phone: a header, one row of chips and a four-line message area. The chips stay in one row that scrolls sideways, as the harmony strip does, so an add never moves the live palette (§3.4). But the live palette already shows the role paints, so the role chips repeat them.
  • A wash is placed by order, not by colour, because the dataset’s wash colours are too light to compare.
  • New words to learn: Extras, wash rung, highlight rung. The menu uses them.
  • More to build: the placement rules, the role menu, a new u link parameter, a display-only column for a pinned Secondary, and pins in undo and redo.

Accessibility

  • Layout (§3.4). The chips are one row that scrolls sideways, and the line under them has a reserved height, so an add, a move or a remove never moves the harmony strip or the live palette. The wheel legend keeps a reserved place for “Your paint”. The row scrolls a new or focused chip into view.
  • Names and roles. The strip is a list with the name “Paints I’m using”. Each chip is a button with the paint and its role in its name, for example “Balthasar Gold, in Metal. Move or remove”, and aria-haspopup="menu". The seed chip is plain text: you change the seed in the seed field.
  • Add. + Add a paint opens a search field with role="combobox" and a listbox. ↑ and ↓ move the cursor (aria-activedescendant), Enter picks, Esc closes and puts focus back on + Add a paint. Each option’s name includes where the paint will go, for example “Balthasar Gold, Citadel, Base, metallic. Goes to Metal”.
  • Move and remove. Enter or Space on a chip opens a menu of menuitemradio items; the current place is checked. ↑ ↓ Home End move, Enter picks, Esc closes and puts focus back on the chip. Delete or Backspace on a chip removes the paint. After a remove, focus goes to the next chip (or the one before it), or to + Add a paint.
  • What happened. The line under the strip is a polite live region that is always on the page. It changes only after you add, move, remove, undo, redo or change the harmony, and it says where the paint went and why. Its height is reserved, so a long message does not move the palette.
  • Undo and redo. ↶ and ↷, and Ctrl+Z / Ctrl+Shift+Z (⌘ on a Mac) when the focus is in the studio and not in a text field. Each add, move, remove and harmony switch is one step. After a remove or a Switch, the line under the strip also offers Undo.
  • State and interaction. “Yours” is a state, so it is a badge (--accent on --accent-weak: 4.86:1 light, 5.54:1 dark). Focus and the list cursor are the --focus ring. A pinned column or card never gets a ring.
  • 44px on touch. In the phone frame, the chips, + Add a paint, the menu items, the list rows and the Switch action are all --tap tall (44px). The live palette’s ↶ ↷ stay 32px, as in the app today (§3.6 follow-up 12).
  • Reduced motion. A new chip fades in and pops (220ms) only under prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference. With reduced motion it appears at once. Nothing else moves.
  • Colour is never the only signal. Each chip writes its role in words, and the pinned wheel node is a square, not only a different colour.

D · Pins on the wheel: harmonies that fit

The explorer’s way (persona Sam, UC-10 “Lock a paint, spin the rest”). You pin a colour paint, and it becomes a fixed point on the wheel, at the paint’s own hue. The harmony nodes move; the pins do not. Each pin takes the role whose node is nearest to it. The harmony strip marks the harmonies that fit your pins, with a real ΔE. A metal has no place on the wheel. So a pinned metal goes in the Metal well under the wheel (the Metal column’s pin), and the app says so.

How it works

  1. A hue ring around the disc. Your seed (⚓) and your pins sit on a thin ring around the wheel, each at its own hue. A dull paint sits near the centre of the disc, where 50° is only a few pixels. On the ring, 5° is easy to see.
  2. Each role has a line to the ring. The line ends at the role’s hue. A pin takes the free role whose hue is nearest. The gap between the end of the line and the pin is the fit.
  3. The fit is a real ΔE 2000. The page turns the pin to the role’s hue and measures the ΔE between the two. This counts only the hue gap. The pin keeps its own lightness and strength, because you chose them. On Shades and Monochromatic the harmony sets lightness, so there the fit is the full ΔE.
  4. A hue gap is not a match quality. So it has its own words: on the hue (ΔE ≤ 3.5) · near the hue (ΔE ≤ 10) · off the hue. The §3.2 words (Good, Poor …) stay for matches. Every hue gap shows the full ΔE next to it.
  5. The strip marks each harmony: on the hue for your pins · near it. No mark: no role is near your pins.
  6. Turn to fit (UC-10). One button, or a drag on the wheel, turns the harmony until its roles are nearest to your seed and your pins together. The seed and the pins do not move. Your seed keeps its role as a pinned paint. The button shows only when a turn brings the seed and every pin near their roles (hue gap ΔE ≤ 10). If no turn does, the line says so, and T does not turn.
  7. A metal goes in the Metal well. It does not change the harmony. Its wash, its highlight and its NMM recipe come from the pinned paint, so Balthasar Gold gets bronze rungs, not steel ones.

The app has no pins and no fit today. Every pin, every fit mark, every turn and every number on a pinned role is a projection. The page makes them in your browser with the committed engine’s own functions: roleIdeals(), deltaE2000(), nearestPaint() and the ladder steps of §7, with the pinned paint’s hex as the role’s ideal. The paints of the roles that you do not pin are the engine’s own results.

    What to build first, and what later

    1. Release 1 · pins (the general model).
      • scheme.js: buildScheme(…, { pins: { Primary | Secondary | Accent | Metal: paint id } }). Pins are taken first (into usedIds), so no other role reuses them. A pinned role’s match is the pinned paint, with pinned: true and its distance (ΔE from the pin to the role’s ideal), never ΔE 0. Its ladders, its NMM recipe and its temperature ladder start from the pin’s hex. About 25 lines and their tests.
      • app.js: state.pins in paletteSnap and applySnap (one undo step for each pin, unpin and change). renderLive resolves the pins too, and adds the display-only Secondary column. schemeGaps, the shopping list and the Accessibility tab read the pinned paint, not idealHex.
      • share.js: one pin parameter, role.paint-id.HEX with _ between pins. An unknown id gives the nearest paint to the hex and the “pick replaced” note. A link without pin opens as today.
      • picker.js: a factory (its element ids, a type filter, an onPick), so the seed field and the pin sheet are one component. It shipped today (c5ec943), so this change needs its tests to stay green.
      • ui.js: the card action row on all four cards (📌 Pin a paint… · Change… · Unpin), the 📌 dock and the pin chip, “The harmony’s own pick”, and rung ΔE on a pinned role. The Metal pin is the headline in the release notes.
    2. Release 2 · help around the pins. The Auto explainer and the family quick-picks on the Metal card; where each row goes; the Switch offer; the fit marks on the harmony strip.
    3. Later. A “📌 Pin a paint I use” entry near the live palette that routes by type (B’s front door); pins on a ladder rung, mainly the wash (Agrax Earthshade); the wheel keys P and Delete (D).
    4. Separate decisions. Bronze #996E46 as a fourth Auto metal (a §7 constant, a CHANGELOG line and a dataset or app version bump); the metal ladder’s highlight step and the gold NMM shadow (§7 constants); the Citadel shade hexes (a data task, docs/DATA_SOURCING.md).

    How this page computes its numbers

    The page carries the committed engine and the full dataset, so it works as a plain file with no server. The engine is the code of src/js/color.js, harmony.js, data.js, scheme.js, seed.js and share.js from git 8ff5a59, with only the import and export words changed. The dataset is all 3,225 paints of src/data/paints.json 1.6.0.

    • Checked in Node against the committed engine: 2,845 role plans (47 seeds × 10 harmonies × Use my paints off, prefer and only × main and accent seed, plus neutral seeds) give the same paints and the same ΔE; so do 1,500 nearest-paint searches and 200 Equivalents lists.
    • The projection (PIN.scheme(…, {pins}) and PIN.ladder(…, {pin})) is the body of buildScheme with one change: a pinned role uses the pinned paint as its match, and builds its rungs from that paint’s hex. With no pins it gives the same result as buildScheme. The Balthasar Gold rungs were checked against direct calls to adjustHsl and nearestPaint.
    • Not modelled: the order of rows in the app’s seed list beyond the matcher, the wheel’s exact drawing, and anything the app stores (marks, settings). A frame that needs those says so.
