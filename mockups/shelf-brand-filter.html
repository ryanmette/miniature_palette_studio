Shelf brand filter — reclaiming three rows

Today the nine brand chips wrap into three full rows on a phone, pushing the grid below the fold. Three ways to fold them down. In every mock the search / status / type / sort row stays as-is.

A — Brand dropdown in the filter row

The brand filter becomes a native <select> beside "All types" — exactly how the Paints drawer already filters by brand. Three rows → zero.

Cheapest & most consistent. Same control the Paints drawer already uses; native picker UI is ideal on touch; free keyboard/screen-reader support. Cost: switching brands is two taps instead of one, and you can't see the brand list at a glance.

B — "Brands ▾" chip opening a bottom sheet

One compact trigger showing the active brand; tapping opens an overlay listing every brand with its paint count — big touch rows, one tap to switch once open.

Filter by brand

Richest, but the most new machinery. Gains per-brand counts and big targets, but it's a new overlay component to build, test and localise — and §3.5 discourages new picker patterns when a segmented control or select already fits. Two taps to switch, same as A.

C — One-row scrollable chip strip

The chips stay, but confined to a single horizontally scrollable row with edge fades — the exact pattern the harmony picker already uses (.harmscroll). The active chip auto-centres. Three rows → one.

Keeps one-tap switching. On-system precedent (harmony strip), zero new patterns, still shows the brand vocabulary at a glance. Cost: one row remains, and off-screen brands need a swipe to reach.

Recommendation: A — it reclaims everything, matches the Paints drawer exactly, and brand filtering on the Shelf is a set-and-forget choice while bulk-stocking, so two taps is fine. If one-tap brand hopping turns out to matter, C is the on-system fallback.
