The shopping list — five ways to take it to the store

Today the shopping list is a filter of the Shelf: the same colour grid, with only the paints marked “to buy”. That view is good for planning at a desk. This study asks what the list must be when you stand in front of a paint rack with a phone in one hand. It looks at how other apps do it, gives five very different directions, and ends with questions for Ryan.

All the paints, hex values and ΔE numbers on this page come from the app’s own dataset and engine (src/data/paints.json, data.js equivalents). The frames work: tap them.

Page theme

0What we have now

Two parts, and they do not talk to each other:

  • Shelf → status filter → “Shopping list”. The Shelf’s colour grid, with only the to-buy paints shown. It is in hue order, and the names show only in a tooltip.
  • ⋯ → Save the shopping list. It saves palette-shopping-list.txt (app.js doExport). The file has two parts: the recipe for the scheme on screen now, then every to-buy paint, in the order they were marked.

Why it is weak in a store

  • Two lists in one file. The recipe is for the bench. In the store you need only the second part.
  • The order is the order you marked them, not the order of the racks. You walk back and forth.
  • A hex is not a colour you can see. #960C09 tells you nothing at a rack.
  • You cannot tick it. On an iPhone the file goes to the Files app, and it opens read-only.
  • It is a copy. When you buy a paint, the shelf does not know.

The pattern: planning and shopping are two different views of one list

  1. Find itWhich rack, which shelf? Group by brand → line, the way the store does.
  2. Read itThe name, big. The brand and range under it. The colour as a swatch, not a hex.
  3. Tick itOne tap, a 44px target, one hand. Ticked stays in place (no jump).
  4. Swap itSold out, or the store does not carry the brand: what is closest here?
  5. Own itAt the till or at home: the ticked paints become “owned” on the shelf.

AThe rack walk — a checklist in store order

A list, not a grid. It is grouped brand → line, because that is how the racks stand in a store. Each row: a swatch, the name in large type, the line under it, and a tick box. A tick makes the paint owned at once (Ryan’s answer 4); a second tap undoes it. The row stays in place while the list is open (§3.4) and leaves the list the next time you open it. A paint wanted in more than one pot shows a “2 pots” tag (answer 5). This is AnyList’s aisle pattern, with paint racks as the aisles.

For

  • The safest choice: every shopper knows a checklist.
  • No new engine work. The grouping is a sort on brand, line, name.
  • A tick is one write through store.setMark(id, 'owned') and render('marks') — the path the Shelf uses already. No new state to keep.
  • The undo is the same box: a tick by mistake costs one more tap.

Against

  • The tick is the success green with an “Owned” tag, because it is owned now — no new state colour (§3.5).
  • Quantity is new data. Today a mark is owned or want. A count needs a field per want mark in store.js (a data-version bump, and a column in the CSV). One pot stays the default, and the tag shows only from 2.
  • With 2 pots, does one tick mean both? This study says yes (you rarely buy half of a line).
  • Does not help when the pot is not on the rack.

Open question

  • Where does this view live: a third Shelf view, a mode of the Shelf’s “Shopping list” filter, or a page a link opens (§6)?
  • Where do you set the count — on the Recipe’s Add button, in the mark menu, or only on this list?

BNot on the rack — the swap in the store

The list knows the store may not have the pot. You say which brands this store carries; a paint from a brand it does not carry shows its closest pot from a brand it does, with the true ΔE and its label (§2). Any row also has “Not on the rack?” for a sold-out pot. No other app in the study does this, and the engine for it exists already (the Equivalents tab). It is direction C of mockups/buying-assistant.html, moved into the store.

For

  • The one thing a paint tool can do that a notes app cannot. It is the app’s main idea (“ideal vs actual”) at the moment of buying.
  • Honest: Kantor Blue has no good swap in Vallejo (ΔE 10.2, Poor). The list says so, and does not hide it.
  • Brand-agnostic (answer 2): the chips are the brands that are on your list, never a fixed set, and the swaps search all ten brands in the dataset. One view works in a one-brand game store and in a shop with every brand.
  • “This store carries” is a choice per trip; saving it per store is a later option.

Against

  • More on each row. It must stay folded until asked.
  • Finishes: a swap for a wash must be a wash (§7 finish ranking). The dataset’s hex for a wash is the colour on white, so a flat paint can look “Good” for Agrax Earthshade when it is not a swap at all. For a wash, contrast or metallic, the swap list must keep to the same finish class — or say there is none.
  • A swap changes the scheme. Should the swap go back to the scheme, or only to the shelf?

COne list, a “for” label per paint

Ryan’s answer 3: one list, with a way to limit an export by project. So this is not a list per project. Each to-buy paint keeps a small “for” label: the scheme you added it from (the Recipe’s “+ Add N to the shopping list” knows it). The list stays one list in store order (A), and the hand-off panel (§6) gets a “Which paints?” choice: all, or one project. Below: the same list seen by its labels. Many lists is a request on a mini-painting app’s request board (featurebase); a label gets most of that for little cost.

For

  • Answers “why did I put this on the list?” — a real question three weeks later.
  • You can buy for one project this trip, and leave the rest.
  • Much smaller than projects: no saved schemes, no list management. A label is the scheme’s name and its share link, kept with the mark.

Against

  • A scheme has no name today (it lives in the URL). The label needs one: ask on Add (“For: Plague Marine”), or make one from the paint and harmony (“Death Guard Green · triadic”).
  • A paint added by hand on the Shelf has no label. It goes under “No project”.
  • Same data change as the quantity: a field per want mark in store.js.

DThe receipt — a list to print or screenshot

A narrow strip like a till receipt. Each paint: a swatch, the name, the brand and line, and a box for a pen. It has no state and needs no app: print it, or take a screenshot — the screenshot is the file, with the colours in it. Citadel Colour exports a PDF for the same reason.

For

  • Lasts for years and works anywhere — the §2 “portable and durable” principle.
  • Cheap: a print style sheet on view A, so it is not a second view to keep up.
  • Works for someone who shops for you.

Against

  • No ticks go back to the shelf. You mark them owned by hand at home.
  • A printer may not print the swatch colours true (the names carry the meaning).

EThe rack map — the wild one

A picture of the rack, with your paints lit up in place. Here: the Citadel Base range (57 pots in the dataset), drawn in hue order. The real order of a store rack is not published, and it changes from store to store — so this picture is a guess.

For

  • Very fast to read: find the lit pot. It looks like nothing else.
  • A good picture for the first-run guide or the App Store page, even if it never ships as a tool.

Against

  • It works for one brand with one known rack. No source gives the rack order, so it would be invented data — against §2 (“honest about approximation”) and §5 (provenance).
  • Ten brands, ten racks. It does not scale.
  • Probably a trap. Shown so the idea is judged, not to be built.

6From the desk to the store

Ryan’s case: plan at a desktop, then shop with a phone. The list must go from one device to the other. There is no server (§1), so it must go in something: a file, a link, or paper.

The four ways, compared

WayTick in storeColourNote
Link / QRYesYesOpens view A on the phone. The best fit. Six paints ≈ 200 characters of URL.
Print / PDFPenYesView D. What Citadel does.
Text fileNoNoDurable fallback. Fix its contents (below).
Share sheet → NotesBy handNoThe same text, sent to an app. Touch only (CAN_SHARE).

What changes in the file

  • One job per file. The scheme recipe becomes its own file (“Save the recipe”).
  • Grouped brand → line, as on the racks, with the paint count.
  • [ ] boxes — a text box that a pen, a notes app or a Markdown app can tick. (In Markdown, - [ ] is a real checkbox.)
  • No hex. A hex does not help at a rack. Finish words stay (“metallic”), because they tell you which pot.
  • A date, so an old copy is easy to spot.

The hard part: two devices, two shelves

With no server, the desktop and the phone each keep their own shelf — two notebooks that are not connected. The list can go out in a link. But when you tick “bought” on the phone, only the phone’s shelf learns it.

Desktop shelfWhere you plan. Owned + shopping list.
the list, in a link →
← “bought” — how?
Phone shelfWhere you shop. Its own copy, on its own device.

Ryan’s answer 1 is both, so this is a real problem, not an edge case. Option 2 is the fit: the list goes out in a link, and what you bought comes back in a link. Both are the same contract as the share link (share.js), so nothing leaves the two devices but a URL you send yourself.

Rules the link back must keep

  • It only adds “owned”. It never removes a mark and never replaces the shelf (unlike Restore…).
  • It asks first on the desktop: “Mark 3 paints owned? Mephiston Red, Kantor Blue, Ivory” — a link from someone else must not change your shelf without a yes.
  • Opening it twice does nothing new (a paint already owned stays owned).
  • Option 1 — the phone is the shelf. Mark owned on the phone and accept that the desktop is out of date. Simple; fine if you plan on the phone too.
  • Option 2 — a link back (chosen). The phone makes a “bought” link (the same idea, the other way). Open it on the desktop to mark those paints owned. One extra step.
  • Option 3 — the backup file. ⋯ → Back up everything on the phone, Restore… on the desktop. It works today, but it replaces the whole shelf, so it is a poor fit for a few paints.
  • Not an option in v1: sync. It needs a server and accounts (§1 out of scope).

7Verdict and questions

A · Rack walkBuild — the base. Every other direction sits on it.
B · Not on the rackBuild, with A — the reason to use this app and not a notes app.
C · “For” labelBuild, small — one list; the label filters the export (answer 3).
D · ReceiptCheap add-on — a print style sheet on A.
E · Rack mapDo not build — invented data; one brand only.
Recommendation (after Ryan’s answers): A + B together, ticked in the app, opened on the phone from a link or a QR code, with a “bought” link back to the other device. Add a quantity and a “for” label to each to-buy mark (one store.js change for both). D is the print view of A, and the fixed text file is the durable fallback.

The first questions (answered above)
  1. Where does your shelf really live — phone, desktop, or both?This picks option 1, 2 or 3 above, and so how “bought” gets back.
  2. Which stores do you buy in?A game store that carries one or two brands makes B very valuable. A big hobby shop with every brand makes A’s rack order the main thing.
  3. One list, or a list per project?Have you stood in a store and asked “what was this one for?”. If yes, C moves up.
  4. A tick: owned at once, or only after “Done”?This study chose “after Done”, so a tick by mistake does no harm.
  5. Do you need a count (two pots of one base) or a price?Today a paint is on the list or not. A count changes the data model.
  6. Is paper or a screenshot good enough for you?If yes, D alone may be the first step, and A can wait.
