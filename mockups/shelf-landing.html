Edge under the pinned panel — 2 options + a mix Asked 2026-10-26

Once the phone panel is pinned, swatches scroll under it, so something must show where the chrome ends and the paints begin. Today (as built) that is a 14px fade. The options below replace it. Both keep the approved rule that there is no line between the tabs and the panel. The edge is only at the bottom of the whole chrome (tabs + panel + handle), and only while it is pinned. At the top of the page nothing passes under it, so there is no edge. Only the two elevation tokens from §3.4 are used. Each still frame has a 2.5× close-up of its edge. Each option also has a live frame: tap its handle to watch the change at the build's real speed.

Reading the frames. Frames are about 77% of a 390px phone, but the line and shadows are drawn at full size. On a real phone a shadow covers a little less of a swatch row than it does here. A drag opens the panel when you lift your finger, not while it moves. The numbers below come from four independent reviews, which measured pixels in the real app with each option added.

Option 1 — Hard line + short shadow same edge in every pinned state

A 1px --border-strong line with --shadow under it. --shadow is the in-flow chrome tier, the same short soft shadow the cards use. The line uses --border-strong rather than the Studio header's --border, because --border is only 1.19:1 against the background and almost disappears in light mode. You asked for a hard line. The line and shadow move with the panel: under the handle when tucked, and under the open panel when it is pulled down.

What it says: "this is a toolbar", the same in every pinned state. What you get in light: a line you can clearly see (1.40:1) with a short shadow under it (about 6px). At 1× the shadow reads more as a heavier line than as a separate shadow. In dark: only the line. The dark --shadow is 0 1px 2px, so it cannot be seen on the background and only adds a 1px dark underline where paints pass. In dark you get the hard line, but not the drop shadow. Build: the dock always has a transparent 1px bottom border, and only its colour changes when it pins (border-bottom-color, never border-width). The reviews measured that the grid never moves when the panel pins, tucks or opens (§3.4); it sits a constant 1px lower than today. Replace the phone rules .shelfdock.stuck { box-shadow: none } and .shelfdock.stuck::after { opacity: 1 }; do not add rules on top of them.

Desktop is a separate yes/no. You asked about the phone. The pinned desktop bar has --shadow today and no line. Option 1 can stay phone-only, or desktop can get the same line so both match. On desktop the bar would then sit between the header's line above it and the new line below it.

Option 2 — Fade when tucked, shadow when open the edge shows the panel's state Chosen 2026-10-26 · built

Decided (Ryan, 2026-10-26): Option 2, with rounded corners. While the open panel covers paints, its bottom edge gets the Filters sheet's corners (--r-card), mirrored: the sheet rounds its top as it comes up from the bottom, and the panel rounds its bottom as it comes down from the top. The frames below show that. The shadow and corners use the "covers paints" rule (however the panel opened). Nothing shows just after the panel pins, and both turn off while the Filters sheet is open. The corners sit over the page's side margin, so you see them mostly through the shadow's curve. They are clear in light and faint in dark.

Tucked: today's fade. Only the handle row is there, so a light touch is enough. Pulled down or tapped open over the paints: the fade cross-fades (200ms, §3.4) into --shadow-pop. That is the floating-overlay tier the Paints drawer already uses, because the open panel now covers paints. With reduced motion it switches with no animation.

When the shadow shows — one decision for you. You said "dragged or tapped". The rule I would build is: the shadow shows while the open panel covers paints, however it opened. That covers a tap or a drag (on release), a scroll up of more than 60px, and scrolling while the search field has focus (the panel never tucks then). It does not show just after the panel pins, in the first 8px of a scroll, when only the title is under it. The simpler rule (.stuck:not(.tucked)) would flash the big shadow there on every slow scroll down from the top. The reviews measured this as up to 7 frames at full strength. If you want the shadow strictly after a drag or tap, say so. A scroll-up reveal would then keep the fade.

Watch, light: --shadow-pop is purple-tinted (rgba(60,50,120,.18)). Over paints it adds a faint violet haze: greys shift toward blue-violet, up to ΔE 7 at the edge, which is "Loose" on our own §3.2 scale. That pulls against §2 (never tint swatches). The Paints drawer already does the same. A neutral shadow would need a §3.1 token change, so it should be its own decision, not a quiet edit here. Light does get a crisp edge: the shadow starts with a sharp 1px step under the panel. Watch, dark: the shadow cannot be seen on the background (ΔE 1.7). It only darkens the first row and a half of paints, so a peach reads as tan. Tucked and open differ only in how far down the darkening reaches, so in dark the "fade turns into shadow" change is weak.

Build: phone only (inside the ≤520px block), so the desktop bar keeps --shadow. Add box-shadow to the dock's existing transform transition; a second transition declaration would replace it and stop the slide. Give ::after its own opacity transition. Put both in the existing prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference block, because the global reduced-motion rule (*) does not reach ::after. A class set by app.js (grid top above the dock's bottom edge) carries the "covers paints" rule.

Mix — line always, deeper shadow when open optional

Option 1 while tucked (line + --shadow), then line + --shadow-pop while the open panel covers paints. The line gives dark a visible edge over the gaps between paints, which Option 2 cannot. The deeper shadow still says "on top of your paints" in light. It has Option 2's light-mode violet note.

For every option. (1) While the Filters sheet is open, the edge would still draw under the dim layer, and Option 2's shadow would show faintly over the bottom of the tabs. The build would turn the edge off while the sheet is open. (2) The reviews found that, as built, the tucked panel's selected status button left a faint shadow below the tabs, the one place you ruled out a line. That is now fixed in the build: the panel's contents hide once the slide ends. So the clean tucked frames above match the app.

Chosen — phone: pull-down bar (C + handle) · desktop: pinned bar (B) Decision 2026-10-26

Phone (≤520px). The search and filter panel lives under the Studio / Shelf tabs, with no rule between them. It shares the header background, so the tabs, the panel and the handle read as one surface. A drag handle sits at its bottom edge. Scroll down and the panel tucks up under the tabs; only the handle stays. Pull the handle down (or tap it, or scroll up) and the panel slides back out over the grid. It is an overlay, so the swatches do not move (§3.4). Brand · type · sort open in a sheet from Filters.

Desktop (>520px). Direction B. One bar holds search, status, brand, type and sort. It pins under the app header while the grid scrolls (position: sticky) and never hides. There is no sheet and no handle, because there is room for every control inline.

How it would be built. One panel element, two behaviours by breakpoint (CSS + a small script; no new dependency). Phone: the handle is a <button aria-expanded aria-controls="shelfPanel">, so Enter / Space open and close it and screen readers hear the state; drag is extra, never the only way. The panel slides with transform (an overlay, no reflow) and snaps open or shut under prefers-reduced-motion. Desktop: position: sticky under the header. The sort keeps the current default order, and the first-load paint stays whichever paint sorts first. Fade: kept (Ryan, 2026-10-26).

As built (after the design critique). These frames are the approved mock; the build differs on purpose: (1) the handle is hidden at the top (frame 1) — nothing to pull there; (2) the handle chip is always there ("⌕ Search" when nothing is on), so search is findable while tucked; (3) a scroll up reveals the panel only after 60px; (4) every filter change scrolls the first results to just below the dock; (5) the pill is drawn in --text-muted (the mock's --border-strong was 1.4:1, failing WCAG 1.4.11); (6) the title row keeps labelled Import / Export buttons instead of a second ⋯, and the how-to hint stays; (7) an active brand/type uses the badge recipe (weak fill), not an accent border.

A — Fix the jump only

One-line change: focus({ preventScroll: true }). Keyboard focus still goes to the grid (arrow keys and P/U/X work at once), but the page stays at the top. The layout does not change.

Smallest change, no risk. It fixes what you saw. The cost: the swatches still start below the middle of a phone screen, and to change a filter after scrolling you must scroll back up through the grid (more than 600 rows on "All brands").

B — Compact header, pinned search Recommended

Fold the controls into two short rows, and pin them under the app header while the grid scrolls (position: sticky). Row 1: search + Filters (brand · type · sort in a bottom sheet, with a badge that counts the active filters). Row 2: the status filter (All · Owned · To buy), because you use it most. Select stays visible because it is a mode. Import and Export move to a ⋯ menu. The hint shows on first visit only. On desktop the same bar pins too, but brand · type · sort stay inline (there is room) and no sheet is needed.

Best balance. The swatches start at about 30% of the screen (was 62%), and search and status are always one tap away. Brand · type · sort keep the native <select> controls (free keyboard and screen-reader support), only inside a sheet. Sticky does not reflow the grid (§3.4). Cost: brand/type/sort take one more tap on a phone, and the pinned bar uses about 90px of screen while you scroll.

C — Compact header, bar hides as you scroll

The same compact header as B. But the pinned bar slides away when you scroll down, and comes back as soon as you scroll up a little (like the Safari address bar). It slides with transform, so the grid does not reflow. With reduced motion it shows or hides with no animation.

Most room for swatches, more moving parts. Good for long browsing. Cost: about 30 lines of scroll-direction JavaScript, and the bar moves under your finger. That can surprise people, and it must never hide while the search field has focus. If B's pinned bar feels too tall in use, it is easy to move to C later.
