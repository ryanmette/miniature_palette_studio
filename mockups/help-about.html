Help and About — what goes where, and how each opens

Two buttons sit side by side in ⋯ → Help & info. Help leaves the app for a separate page. About & data opens over the app. They overlap in content, and the long Help page has started to drift from the app. This study lists what is there now, the rules we must keep, three directions, and questions for Ryan.

Page theme

0What we have now

Why they are different (on purpose)

  • Help and Privacy are web pages because the App Store needs a public Support URL and a privacy policy that anyone can open in a browser, also without the app (§1 below).
  • About is a dialog because it shows things about this device: the version, your shelf, and Delete my shelf and settings….
  • Nothing in the app says this. So the two buttons look the same and act differently.

The cost of two copies: Help has already drifted

  • Out of date: “A backup holds … your settings: theme, language …”. Language is no longer a setting: it follows the device (2026-10-28).
  • Out of date: “Delete … removes your owned marks, your shopping list and your settings”. It also removes your saved schemes (since item 35).
  • Order, not task: the 13 questions are in the order the features were built. The four store questions sit between “Where is my shelf saved?” and “How do I move my shelf?”.

1The rules we must keep (primary sources)

“People need to know how to reach you with questions and support issues. Make sure your app and its Support URL include an easy way to contact you …”— App Store Review Guidelines, 1.5 Developer Information (developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines)
“All apps must include a link to their privacy policy in the App Store Connect metadata field and within the app in an easily accessible manner.”— App Store Review Guidelines, 5.1.1 (i) Privacy Policies
Support URL: “This URL must lead to actual contact information … This property is required …”— App Store Connect Help, Platform version information
  • So: support.html and privacy.html stay as public web pages, with a way to contact Ryan. Every direction below keeps them.
  • Inside the app, a privacy policy link must be easy to reach, and the app must show a way to make contact.
  • What we can change: how Help opens in the app, what each place says, and the names.

2Common patterns in other apps

  • Help as a web page, opened in a sheet over the app. On iOS, many apps open their help site in an in-app browser sheet (Safari View Controller), so you never leave the app. For us that needs a Capacitor plugin that is not on the allowlist (CLAUDE_NATIVE.md §4), and the web app cannot do it.
  • Help built into the app, with the same text also on the web for the store. The risk is two copies; the fix is one source.
  • About as a short identity page: name, version, credits, licences, legal links. It does not explain how to use the app.

3Three directions

A · Keep the page, make it belong

  • For: the smallest change. Group the questions by task, add a contents list, fix the drift, trim About.
  • Against: Help still leaves the app. In the iPhone app the page replaces the Studio until you press Back.
  • Against: two buttons in ⋯ still act in two ways.

B · Help opens over the app, from one source (recommended)

  • One source: support.html stays the public page and the only copy of the text. The app reads that same file (it is in the offline cache already) and shows its contents in a sheet. No build step, no second copy, so no drift.
  • For: Help and About open the same way. You never leave the Studio, on the web or in the iPhone app.
  • For: a link such as “How the matching works” opens Help at that section, in place.
  • Against: a little more code: the sheet, and the in-page links inside it. Two places show the text (page and sheet), so the page's styles must suit both.
  • About becomes short: what the app is, the version, data & credits, fonts, your shelf + Delete, links to Help and Privacy. The matching text moves to Help only.

C · One “Help & about” sheet with tabs

  • For: one entry point, one place to look. The ⋯ row has one button.
  • Against: three kinds of content in one sheet. Privacy in a tab is still the full policy, which is long.
  • Against: the footer's “About & data” link then opens the same sheet on a tab, which is less direct.
  • It needs B's one-source idea too, or the Help tab is a second copy.

Help, grouped by task

  1. Start a scheme: Roll a scheme and ↻; keep a colour or a pot (holds); Lightness.
  2. Matching and ΔE: the labels; Use my paints; why a match does not look exact.
  3. Your shelf: mark paints; the list; where it is saved; delete it.
  4. Schemes: save, open, rename, share.
  5. Shopping and the store: the list in a store; not on the rack; to the phone and back; counts and print.
  6. Backup and other devices: back up, restore, import a paintRack list; offline.
  7. Keyboard and Contact and privacy.

Names

  • ⋯ group: Help & info → keep. Buttons: Help and About (drop “& data”: the credits are one part of it).
  • The page title: Help (not “Help and support”). The footer link: About.

5Verdict and questions

A · Keep the pageSmall — fixes the content, not the jump out of the app.
B · Sheet from one sourceRecommended — same behaviour as About, no second copy, no drift.
C · One tabbed sheetMaybe later — tidy, but mixes three kinds of content.
Recommendation: B. Help opens in a sheet over the app, built from support.html itself, so the public page (for the App Store) and the app always say the same thing. Regroup Help by task, fix the two drifted facts, and make About a short identity page with Data & credits and your shelf. Privacy stays a page, linked from both.
