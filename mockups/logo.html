What the mark must do

  1. Say "miniature paint", not "an app". A painter should recognise an object from the bench.
  2. Survive 16 px. One clear silhouette. Detail can come back at 28 px and up.
  3. Work in one colour. The app icon and forced-colours mode have only one ink.
  4. Not be a letter in a box. The "P" plaque was one of the audit's "made by AI" signs.
  5. Not copy a brand. No Citadel pot, no Games Workshop shapes, no trade dress.
  6. Agree with the system. The Workbench+ icons (square ends, 1.5 px at 20 px) and, if Ryan takes them, round pot-lid swatches.

Recommendation: 4a, the dropper and lid, if Ryan says yes to round pot-lid swatches. Reasons:

  • It is the only mark that says both "hobby paint" (the bottle) and what the app does (the colour you pick becomes a pot).
  • It has no droplet, which Ryan was unsure about.
  • It uses the same round lid as every paint swatch, so the logo and the UI teach each other.

If Ryan likes the drop after all: 4b. It is the painter judge's choice and the most natural action.

If the swatches stay square: the lid in 4a loses its echo in the UI. Then 4b, or 2c (target and pot) for a calm, abstract mark.

What the build does with the chosen mark

  • src/icon.svg: the browser tab icon and the installed-app icon. Its ink switches to the dark-theme cream with prefers-color-scheme inside the SVG (Chrome and Firefox follow this). The mark keeps 10% clear on each side, so Android's circle crop never cuts it.
  • A PNG fallback on a teal tile for Safari, which does not use SVG tab icons.
  • The header in index.html, support.html and privacy.html: an inline SVG in place of the "P" plaque, aria-hidden (the wordmark is the name).
  • native/assets: the 1024 px icon and the two splash screens are made from the same SVG.
