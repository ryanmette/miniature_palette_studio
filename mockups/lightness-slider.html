Lightness slider — four directions

The problem (Ryan, 2026-10-27): the slider under the wheel has no label, so “it’s not intuitive what it does”.

What it does (wheel.js:230): it sets the lightness of your colour, from 6% to 94% (HSL L). The wheel redraws at that lightness, and the whole scheme gets lighter or darker, because the other colours share it. The ↻ button next to it picks a random hue and strength, and keeps the lightness.

What Apple says (HIG · Sliders): “Consider using a label to introduce a slider.” “A slider can optionally display left and right icons that illustrate the meaning of the minimum and maximum values.” And customise the track, the thumb and the icons “to blend with your app’s design and communicate intent”.

Every slider on this page works. Drag it, or use the arrow keys, and the wheel and your colour change, as in the app.

Today

A native range with the accent colour. Nothing says what it changes. On a phone the control is 16px tall (measured at 390px), well under the 44px touch rule (§3.6), and the ↻ icon can read as “reload” or “undo”.

A · Label it

A small “Lightness” label and the value as a percentage, above the slider on a phone and over it on desktop. The slider itself does not change.

Pro: the smallest change, and the word is explicit. Con: “Lightness 24%” still does not say of what. It adds a row on a phone (about 16px). The slider stays 16px tall on a phone.

B · Show it

The track paints your colour from dark to light, and the thumb is filled with your colour now. A dark dot and a light dot mark the two ends (Apple’s “left and right icons”). One short caption names it. You can see what the slider does before you touch it. It is still a native range, so the keyboard and screen readers work as today.

Pro: it explains itself without reading, in both themes, and it shows where the colour will go. The touch area grows to 44px. Con: it needs custom CSS for the track and the thumb in WebKit and Firefox. The track redraws with each colour change (a CSS gradient, so it is cheap).

C · Big track, label inside

A 44px-tall track, like the iOS Control Center brightness slider, with the label inside it. You can grab it anywhere. The track shows your colour from dark to light, and a thin bar marks the value.

Pro: the biggest touch target and the clearest label. Con: the heaviest look under the wheel. The label sits on top of the colour, so it needs its own small chip to stay readable. On desktop, a 44px-wide vertical bar takes more of the gutter.

D · Tone steps

Seven swatches of your colour, from dark to light. Tap one to use it. Painters already think in tones, and each step shows its result. This is a radio group, so the arrow keys move between the steps.

Pro: no words needed, and it reads like a paint ladder. Con: only 7 values instead of 89 steps, so fine control is lost. It also looks like the live palette and the Plan’s tone ladders, so it can be confused with them.

And the ↻ button

↻ can read as “reload” or “undo”. Its name is “Generate a random base colour”, but only screen readers and a hover tooltip say so. Three options, all 44px:
