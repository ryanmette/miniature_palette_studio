Lightness slider — four directions

The problem (Ryan, 2026-10-27): the slider under the wheel has no label, so “it’s not intuitive what it does”.

Result: three judges chose B. See the recommendation, the scores, the bugs found on the way and the docs to change.

What it does (wheel.js:230): it sets the lightness of the scheme’s base colour, from 6% to 94% (HSL L). The wheel redraws at that lightness. The harmony colours share the base lightness, so they change with it. Added colours, detached colours and the neutral pop keep their own lightness, so they do not change. The ↻ button next to the slider picks a random hue and strength. It keeps the lightness.

What Apple says (HIG · Sliders): “Consider using a label to introduce a slider.” “A slider can optionally display left and right icons that illustrate the meaning of the minimum and maximum values.” And customise the track, the thumb and the icons “to blend with your app’s design and communicate intent”.

Every slider on this page works. Drag it, or use the arrow keys, and the wheel and your colour change, as in the app. The hero starts on Alga Green (#555E1B, as in paints.json). When you move a slider, the hero shows “Custom” and the new hex, as in the app. It does not change back to the paint.

Scale: the desktop frames in Today and A–D are not to scale. They show a 240px wheel in a 520px frame, so the gutter looks roomy. In the app at 1025px, the wheel is 340px and the wheel row has only 6px free. The recommended desktop frame uses the real sizes.

Today

A native range with the accent colour. Nothing says what it changes. On a phone the control is 16px tall (measured at 390px in Chromium), well under the 44px touch rule (§3.6). On iOS the native thumb (about 28px) is the real target. The ↻ icon can read as “reload”, or as “redo” next to the live palette’s ↶ and ↷.

A · Label it

A small “Lightness” label and the value as a percentage, above the slider on a phone and over it on desktop. The slider itself does not change.

Pro: the smallest change, and the word is explicit. Con: “Lightness 24%” still does not say of what. It adds a row on a phone (about 16px). The slider stays 16px tall on a phone.

Review notes

  • The d20 button is a separate change from the slider. Judge the slider alone. See “And the ↻ button”.
  • “24%” is HSL lightness. A painter cannot act on a bare percentage (§2, principle 4).
  • In the app at 1025px, the 53px “Lightness” header does not fit the 38px gutter.
  • On the phone, the value sits above the Random button, not above the slider, so it reads as part of the button.

B · Show it

The track paints your colour from dark to light, and the thumb is filled with your colour now. A dark dot and a light dot mark the two ends (Apple’s “left and right icons”). One short caption names it. You can see what the slider does before you touch it. It is still a native range, so the keyboard and screen readers work as today. (Two limits: forced colours remove the custom track and the dots, and this frame has no Firefox rule for the vertical track. The recommendation fixes both.)

Pro: it explains itself without reading, and it shows where the colour will go. The touch area grows to 44px. Con: it needs custom CSS for the track and the thumb in WebKit and Firefox. The end dots and the thumb ring fail contrast (see the notes). The track redraws with each colour change (a CSS gradient, so it is cheap).

Review notes

  • The d20 button is a separate change from the slider. Judge the slider alone. See “And the ↻ button”.
  • The end dots fail contrast. The light dot on the light card is 1.08:1. The dark dot on the dark card is 1.02:1. So each dot disappears in one theme. The recommendation removes the dots.
  • The thumb fails WCAG 1.4.11 at the light end. In the light theme, a 3px white border and a 35%-black hairline give about 2.4:1 against a near-white track. The recommendation uses the wheel’s black-or-white ring (4.28:1 or more).
  • The dots and the word “Random” shorten the phone track to 129px in this frame (about 173px on a real 390px phone, against 262px today).
  • The caption is centred under the whole row, not under the track. It also sits on the P/A/2 legend, so it reads as part of the legend.

C · Big track, label inside

A 44px-tall track, like the iOS Control Center brightness slider, with the label inside it. You can grab it anywhere. The track shows your colour from dark to light, and a thin bar marks the value.

Pro: the biggest touch target and the clearest label. Con: the heaviest look under the wheel. The label sits on top of the colour, so it needs its own small chip to stay readable, and the chip then hides the value. On desktop, a 44px-wide vertical bar takes more of the gutter.

Review notes

  • The d20 button is a separate change from the slider. Judge the slider alone. See “And the ↻ button”.
  • The label chip hides the thumb for L from about 7% to 56%. Most paints are in that range, so you cannot see the value. At the 24% start, the chip is on the thumb now.
  • On iOS a range moves only from its thumb. This thumb is 8px wide, so “grab it anywhere” is not true on an iPhone.
  • The chip has its own shadow. It is not one of the two shadow tokens (§3.4).
  • The model is the Control Center brightness slider. A painter can think that the slider dims the screen.

D · Tone steps

Seven swatches of your colour, from dark to light. Tap one to use it. Painters already think in tones, and each step shows its result. This is a radio group, so the arrow keys move between the steps.

Pro: no words needed, and it reads like a paint ladder. Con: only 7 values instead of 89 steps, so fine control is lost. It also looks like the live palette and the Plan’s tone ladders, so it can be confused with them.

Review notes

  • The d20 button is a separate change from the slider. Judge the slider alone. See “And the ↻ button”.
  • The phone tones are 20–21px wide. §3.6 needs 44px.
  • Selection is not reliable. A tone is selected when the lightness is within 0.061 of it, and the steps are 0.12 apart. At a midpoint (for example 30%), two tones are selected. Most paints are between steps, so no tone is selected. You cannot go back to the paint’s own lightness.
  • The selected ring is the same as the focus ring. A keyboard user cannot tell selection from focus (§3.5).
  • The steps cover 12–84%. The slider covers 6–94%.
  • The arrow keys do not wrap at the ends (the APG radio group pattern wraps). Desktop tones are 44×28px. The lightest and darkest step edges are 1.22:1 and 1.27:1 against the card.

And the ↻ button

↻ can read as “reload”. In the app it is also near the live palette’s ↶ undo and ↷ redo buttons. ↻ and ↷ look almost the same, so ↻ can read as “redo”. Its name is “Generate a random base colour”, but only screen readers and a hover tooltip say so. Three options, all 44px:

On a phone there is room for the word next to the slider. On desktop the gutter is narrow, so the icon stays alone there, with its tooltip and accessible name. All three judges chose option 3 on a phone and option 2 on desktop. Two judges note that a 20px d20 can look like a hexagon or a gem. Test it with painters, and test a d6 with pips against it.

Recommendation · B, fixed Chosen by 3 of 3

All three judges chose B. These frames show B with the fixes that the judges agree on. The track shows your colour from dark to light. A visible word names the slider. The end dots are removed. A black or white ring keeps the thumb visible at every lightness. Drag a slider, or use the arrow keys. The screen-reader text under each frame changes with it.

How it fits at 1025px. At 1025px the studio column gives the wheel row 398px. The row is a 340px wheel + a 12px gap + a 38px gutter = 390px, so 8px stays free. (Today the gap is 14px, which is off the spacing scale, and 6px stays free.) The 38px gutter is the input box, at the desktop --tap. The 12px track and the 28px thumb sit inside it. “Lighter” and “Darker” are centred on the gutter, so they do not make it wider. The word “Lightness” (53px) does not fit, so on desktop it stays the accessible name and the tooltip. At 1180px the column is 468px, so there is more room.

What to build

  • Control: one native <input type="range" min="6" max="94" step="1">. The arrow keys, Page Up, Page Down, Home and End work as today.
  • Name: a visible <label for> “Lightness” above the track, at the left (phone). The label is the accessible name. Remove the aria-label, so there is no second name. On desktop the label is visually hidden, and the gutter shows “Lighter” above the track and “Darker” below it.
  • Track: 12px thick, pill radius. It shows the pick’s own hue and strength from L 6% to 94% (five stops). JS writes only colours on the input (never on :root): --wl-stops, --wl-thumb and --wl-ring. Write them in the wheel’s shared draw() path, not in the slider’s input handler, so they also update after a pick, a typed hex, undo, Random and a share link. Cache the stops by an h:s key (about 0.014ms per write). CSS sets the direction: to the right on a phone, to the top in the gutter. A 1px var(--text-muted) edge shows where the track ends (4.68:1 light, 6.37:1 dark).
  • Thumb: 28px, filled with the colour now. Its 3px ring uses the wheel’s textOn rule (#15150F or #FFFFFF). Worst case against the fill and the track under it: 4.28:1. Worst case against the card: 4.28:1 light, 4.12:1 dark. The token ring (2px surface + 1px text-muted) falls to 2.16:1 on some mid colours, so we do not use it.
  • Hit area: the input box is var(--tap) thick: 44px on a phone or a touch screen, 38px with a mouse.
  • Track length: at 390px the phone track is about 207px (262px today). At 360px it is about 182px, which is under the painter judge’s 200px. The word “Random” costs about 60px. Test the drag on a 360px phone.
  • Value: aria-valuetext gives a number and a word, for example “24%, dark”. Bands: very dark (up to 15), dark (up to 35), mid (up to 65), light (up to 85), very light. There is no number on screen. The hero hex shows the exact result.
  • Focus: outline: 2px solid var(--accent), offset 3px (5.70:1 light, 6.20:1 dark).
  • Forced colours: appearance: auto in @media (forced-colors: active) gives the system slider back.
  • Motion and layout: nothing animates. The gradient and the ring change by paint only. Nothing reflows.
  • Random: d20 + “Random” on a phone, d20 alone on desktop. aria-label “Random colour”. title “Random colour (keeps the lightness)”. The glyph is aria-hidden. Secondary button style, var(--tap) high.
  • Source colour: paint the track and the thumb from the pick (the hero colour). It shares L with the scheme base, so the thumb matches the hero in accent-seed mode too.

Why B

  • You see what it does before you touch it. The track is your own colour from dark to light. All three judges gave this as the main reason.
  • It stays a native range. The keys, screen readers, undo and share links work as today.
  • The hit area is 44px on a phone. Today the control is 16px tall in Chromium.
  • It is cheap at run time. One custom-property write costs about 0.014 ms (measured by the build judge).
  • Its problems are all fixable without a change to the idea: the end dots, the thumb ring and the off-token chrome.

Bugs found on the way

These bugs are in the app today, not in this mockup. Fix them in the same build.

  1. The round trip drifts. Move the slider to 6% and back, and the colour changes. Measured in the app, in one jump: #555E1B → 6% → 24% = #535F1C, and #6B5B4B → 6% → 36% = #6B594D. Step by step (arrow keys, as a drag does): #555E1B comes back as #575E1C, and #6B5B4B as #6C4B4B (the hue moves from 30° to 0°, brown to red). Cause: wheel.js:230 reads the hue and the saturation again from the 8-bit hex on each input event. Fix: hold h and s while the slider moves. Add a node --test for the round trip. The sliders on this page hold h and s, so they do not drift.
  2. A lightness drag can start neutral mode. At the ends of the slider, the seed’s chroma falls below C* 10, so the studio goes into neutral mode. This is by design (§7). Alga Green has C* 9.4 at 6% and C* 8.7 at 94%. Three test colours all showed the neutral overlay at 6%. This is a design question: should a lightness drag change the mode?
  3. The slider’s focus ring is almost invisible. It uses the app-wide var(--focus) ring (--accent-weak): 1.17:1 in the light theme and 1.12:1 in the dark theme. Fix: outline: 2px solid var(--accent), offset 3px, as on the wheel canvas.
  4. Touch screens wider than 520px get small targets. --tap is 44px only at 520px or less. So a touch tablet gets a 26px-wide slider and a 38px ↻. Fix: set --tap to 44px for (pointer: coarse) too, and make the slider box var(--tap) wide. At 1025px the row is then 340 + 12 + 44 = 396px of 398px.
