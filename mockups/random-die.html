Glyph study

Five glyphs on the app’s secondary button (44px), at 16, 20, 24 and 28px. Each is inline SVG in currentColor (so it takes --text in both themes) and aria-hidden: the button’s name does the talking. The app’s ↻ is 15px today. Checked in Chromium at 1× (a desktop monitor) and 3× (a phone).

Which still reads as a die at 20px

  • (d) the d6 with five pips reads as a die at every size, on a 1× and a 3× screen. Nothing else has a rounded square with pips. The small tilt makes it a thrown die, not a keypad or a grid icon. It is also the most on-brand die for this audience: Warhammer and most wargames roll buckets of d6. The d20 is the role-playing die.
  • (c) is the d20 that still reads at 20px. The strokes are thicker (2.2 to 2.4 units) and there are only five facet lines, so they do not merge. The shaded front face gives it volume: it reads as a solid object, not as a flat hexagon. At 16px it is a hexagon with a triangle in it, and to a wargamer it can look like a hex nut or a warning sign. The face is now at fill-opacity .5 (it was .35, under 3:1).
  • (a), the earlier d20, works at 24px and up. At 16px on a 1× screen its nine thin facet lines merge into a grey knot. At 20px it is busy: two judges saw a knot, one saw a faceted hexagon that still reads. This is the “hexagon or gem” risk the judges named.
  • (b) the “20” needs 24px and a 2× or 3× screen. At 20px the number is about 4px tall and turns to a smudge. When you can read it, it is the strongest d20 cue, because a “natural 20” is what painters and gamers know.
  • (e) ↻ reads as an arrow at every size. That is the problem: it says “reload” or “redo”, not “random”. It is also a text character, not an SVG: the self-hosted Inter does not hold it, so each device draws it with its own system font (see the font note in Today).

Contrast of each glyph (WCAG 1.4.11, 3:1 for a graphic)

Each glyph is in currentColor, which is --text on the secondary button. The strokes and pips are 15.54:1 on the light surface (13.98:1 on the hover fill) and 13.89:1 on the dark surface (12.83:1 on hover), for (a), (b), (d) and (e). The only part with less contrast is the shaded face of (c): 3.12:1 light (3.05:1 on hover) and 4.39:1 dark (4.25:1 on hover) at fill-opacity .5, and 4.98:1 light / 3.16:1 dark against its own stroke. At .35 it was 2.11:1 light and 2.80:1 dark, which failed. So all five pass, and the choice between them is about shape, not contrast.

The button, its job, its name and its place do not change. At rest the icon is ↻. When you tap it, the colour changes at once, as today. B · ↻ stays for the colour; a new d6 rolls a whole scheme

↻ keeps its job: a random base colour. A new Roll a scheme button with a d6 picks a random harmony and a random hue and strength, in one step. The scheme changes on the tap, while the die turns in place. Try both buttons in any frame, and move the d6 to each place with the control below. (The judges scored a version where the scheme changed when the die landed, 380ms later.)

It changes
The harmony: one of the nine that have a rule, never Custom and never the one you have. The base hue and strength: as ↻ (hue 0–360°, strength 50–95%). So the hero turns “Custom”: the paint you picked goes.
It keeps
The lightness (your value choice, as ↻ keeps it). The seed role (Main or Accent). Use my paints and the tone ladder. Locked colours stay. Added colours turn with the base, as with ↻.
Undo
One ↶ step undoes the harmony and the colour together. Each roll is its own step (quick ↻ taps merge; rolls do not).
Neutral seed
Not designed yet. On a black or white seed the roll cannot give a hue (as ↻). Near the slider’s ends most rolls stay neutral: 79% at 6%, 88% at 94%. Then the app’s ensureHarmonyMode puts Neutral + pop back and parks the rolled harmony, so “Rolled: Triadic” would be false, and the parked harmony comes back later, unasked, when the seed leaves neutral mode.
Option: harmony only
The painter judge’s fix (not scored): the roll keeps your paint and changes only the harmony. It is the same path as a tap on a harmony chip (render('scheme'): one ↶ step, the strip and the hero stay in step). A neutral seed rolls only from the harmonies it can use (Neutral + pop, Duotone, Warm–cool, Shades), so the announcement is always true. Try it with the control below.

Verdict

Decision (Ryan, 2026-10-28): the die is a d6. The d20 may come back later as an option, for example a choice for role-playing gamers. Ryan then chose B: keep ↻ and add the “Roll a scheme” d6.

The scores support C with the d6 (d). C has 46 of 60; the next direction, Today, has 35. The d6 has 51 of 60; the next glyph, (c), has 40. Neither result is close: every judge put C first and the d6 first.

C goes against Ryan’s request. C replaces ↻, and Ryan asked to keep ↻. So this page does not recommend C, unless Ryan changes the request (open question 1).

The recommendation that keeps ↻

The options that keep ↻ are close, and none of them beats today: Today 35, B 33, A 32. Two judges (the painter and accessibility) named B in the seed toolbar as the best way to keep ↻. The build judge’s main objections to B come from the roll that also changes the colour. So:

  1. Keep ↻ as it is. Its job, place, size and name do not change. Do not build A’s tumble: two judges said no, and it does not fix the meaning at rest.
  2. Add B in the seed toolbar only, with the words “Roll a scheme” and the d6. Never as an icon alone, and not in the gutter or the harmony strip.
  3. Make it roll the harmony only. It keeps the painter’s paint. Then each button has its own job: ↻ gives a new colour, and the die gives a new scheme for your colour. Try it in B with “Harmony only”.

This is close, and the last step is not scored. It reads the judges’ written advice. On the scores alone, no change (Today, 35) is a little ahead of B (33) and A (32). And ↻ still reads as “reload” at rest, because only C fixes that.

What to build if Ryan agrees

Place
The seed toolbar. Phone (≤520px): row 1, after ☰ Paints (row 1 holds only Paints today; the hex field fills row 2). Desktop: after Paints, before the hex field.
Name
The visible words “Roll a scheme” are the accessible name: no aria-label (WCAG 2.5.3). title “Roll a scheme: a random harmony for this colour”. Both through data-i18n (en-US: “color”). The words never name the die, so a d6 or d20 change needs no new strings. The Help page (support.html) says what the roll changes and keeps, because a title does not show on touch.
Glyph
The d6 (d): inline SVG, 20px, currentColor, aria-hidden="true", focusable="false".
Size
The secondary .btn, --tap high: 38px with a mouse, 44px on touch and at ≤520px. Padding 0 12px 0 10px. No new button style.
What it does
It picks a harmony at random from the ones the seed can use, never Custom and never the current one. A seed with a hue: the nine with a rule. A neutral seed: Neutral + pop, Duotone, Warm–cool and Shades. It then runs the harmony chip’s own path: state.harmony, state.dropOffsets = [], the strip’s aria-pressed, scrollHarmonyActive(), render('scheme'). So each roll is one ↶ step, the hero chip pops and the live region speaks the new scheme. It keeps the pick, the seed role, the lightness, the pop, the locked and added colours, and Use my paints.
Code
A pure rollHarmony(rng, current, neutral) in harmony.js, with unit tests (node --test). The click handler in app.js. No new file.
Motion
The die hops 5px and turns once: 320ms (the §3.4 card-reveal tier), cubic-bezier(.34,1.56,.64,1), transform only, with element.animate(). The scheme changes on the tap. Nothing waits for the animation.
Reduced motion
The script checks matchMedia('(prefers-reduced-motion: reduce)') itself (the CSS rule does not stop element.animate()). No turn; the scheme changes at once.
↻
No change to its look or its job. Two small fixes the judges found: give it data-i18n-aria and a title key (en-US users get “colour” today), and decide what it does on a black or white seed (open question 4).
