Keep this, change the rest: your starting paint, locks and pins

How the Studio should let a painter say what must stay while the app works out the rest. The study measures today's controls, looks at how other tools do it, proposes one model, and walks three painters through it.

“Let's do a study on how the flow would work. The pinning vs locking and everything else. Maybe look outside for influences to better achieve this.” — and: “‘Make this your paint’ can feel confusing if it's not a paint you already own.” (Ryan)

1. Today, measured

Finding: a lock never reaches the Recipe.

Measured in the live build (v1.11.0, Chrome, 1280px), starting colour #4A6B2F, Triadic:

  1. Before: live palette and Recipe agree. Secondary is #2F4A6B in both.
  2. Lock Secondary: the column jumps after Accent. It is now a “free” colour, cut from the harmony.
  3. Change to Complementary: the palette keeps the blue but calls it “Added”; the Recipe's Secondary becomes green #2F6B32. The shopping list follows the Recipe, so the lock bought nothing.
  4. ↻ and Roll behave the same. After a Roll to Shades the palette has 7 columns; the Recipe still has its 4 roles and no blue.

Why: currentScheme() (app.js) builds the Recipe from the starting colour, the harmony and the settings only. Locks, edits and added colours live in state.extraNodes, which only the live palette reads. So today the app has two models of one scheme. A pin added on top would be a third.

Finding: “your paint” collides with “my paints”.

Since v1.11.0 the pick is “your paint”, and ⋯ has “Use my paints”, which means owned. A painter who picks Mephiston Red to plan a new army does not own it, yet the app calls it “your paint” and the Shelf's menu says “Make this your paint”. Both words claim ownership. (Ryan's note.)

The case the app cannot do

Ryan's Death Guard example, computed with the app's engine (data 1.6.0): starting from Citadel Death Guard Green #6D774D, the Metal role's target is gunmetal #6E7177 in Complementary, Triadic and Analogous alike, matched to Vallejo Dark Steel (ΔE 2.5). The painter wants Balthasar Gold #A77353: ΔE 22.9 from that target, a Poor match. Nothing in the app can hold it there.

What carries over

1 · One marked anchor

Adobe's base, M3's source, Spotify's seed. Everything follows it. Ours is the starting paint; it needs a clear mark and a name that does not mean “owned”.

2 · The fix lives on the item

Coolors, Realtime Colors, Fontjoy: the padlock sits on what it keeps, and pressing it again undoes it. No mode, no settings page.

3 · Two kinds of fix

M3 separates colours that may be nudged to fit from colours that must stay exact. Ours: a locked colour is a target (the nearest pot may change); a pinned pot is literal (it is the paint on your desk).

4 · The fix is per role

Huemint knows background, foreground and accent. Our roles (Primary, Secondary, Accent, Metal) are the natural slots for a fix; a free “Added” colour that belongs to no role is what broke today.

5 · Free colours fit the fixed ones

Realtime Colors regenerates “in relation to” the locks. For us the harmony still comes from the anchor, but the report on fit must account for the fixes.

6 · Name the clash

Solver's Feasibility Report and Onshape's red constraint: when a fix makes the result poor, say which fix and how far. Never silently degrade.

Vocabulary: lock (padlock) is the near-universal word for “keep when I reroll”. Base / source / seed is the one input everything comes from. Pin means “keep at the top” almost everywhere; no tool uses it for rerolls, so if we use it, the UI must teach it by what it holds (a pot).

3. One model: what you decide, what the app works out

Every control sorts into one of four jobs. The model fixes the finding above by making the live palette and the Recipe two views of one scheme.

You decideStartThe starting paint (or colour), and the role it plays: Primary or Accent.
You decideHold🔒 Lock a role's colour (a target) or 📌 pin a role's pot (a real paint). Held things survive every reroll.
You askExploreHarmony, Roll, ↻, drag, lightness. Each one recomputes everything you have not held.
You limitFilterUse my paints, Include Contrast, the tone ladder. They limit which pots may match; they never move a colour.

Three consequences:

  • A hold belongs to a role. Locking the Secondary column holds the Secondary target, in the palette and in the Recipe. The shopping list follows.
  • A pin is a lock plus a chosen pot. Pinning Balthasar Gold to Metal sets the Metal target to its colour and the Metal pot to it. Unpinning gives the generated target back.
  • Extra colours stay extra. A colour added with + that is not a role (a fifth or sixth column) is a sketch colour: it shows on the palette as Extra, it can be locked, and it is not in the Recipe. The palette says so, so nobody expects to buy it.

5. Three directions for the whole flow

A · One scheme, two holds Recommended

Fix the model first: the live palette and the Recipe show one scheme. Each role can be locked (hold its colour) or have a chosen pot (hold the paint). For
  • Fixes today's lock bug and adds the pin in one model.
  • Keeps Sam's colour exploration (lock) and Priya's planning (choose pot).
  • Each hold sits on the thing it holds, in both views (Coolors, Huemint).
  • Small engine change: buildScheme takes per-role holds; the pin study's prototype did it in about 12 ms.
Against
  • Two marks to learn (lock and pot). Words and the fit line must carry it.
  • Locks on today's “Added” columns need a migration: role columns keep their lock; extra columns become Extra.

B · Choose pots only

One kind of hold. The palette's lock goes; to keep a role, you choose its pot (the nearest pot is offered first, one tap). A held role is always a real paint.

For
  • One concept, one icon. Nothing to explain.
  • Everything held is buyable, so the shopping list is exact.
Against
  • Sam can no longer keep a colour that no pot matches well (an exact teal for a cloak); he must accept a pot.
  • Removes a control people know from Coolors and from today's app.
  • Locking a target and letting “Use my paints” pick the owned pot later is lost.

C · A paint list first (“Paints I'm using”)

The pin study's direction B, widened: a tray of paints you are using. The first is the starting paint; the app assigns roles to the rest and fills the gaps. Locks stay on the palette for colour play.

For
  • The most hobby-native: it starts from the pots on the desk. No hobby app does this today.
  • No need to know roles before choosing paints.
Against
  • The largest change: a new surface and role assignment rules (a gold is Metal, but a brown could be Secondary or Metal).
  • Two places to set the scheme (the tray and the wheel). It scored 35 of 60 in the pin study, against A's 49.

6. The rules (direction A)

What each action does to each kind of decision. Keeps means untouched; recomputes means the app works it out again.

ActionStarting paint🔒 Locked colour📌 Chosen potRoles you did not hold
Pick a new starting paintReplacedKeepsKeepsRecomputes
⚓ Move here (Primary ⇄ Accent)Changes roleKeepsKeepsRecomputes
Harmony chip · Roll a schemeKeepsKeepsKeepsRecomputes
↻ Pick a colour for me · wheel drag · lightnessNew colourKeepsKeepsRecomputes
Use my paints · Include ContrastKeepsKeeps the colour; its pot may changeKeeps the pot (“not in your paints” under Only)Pots may change
Undo / redoEach hold, release and change is one step, as today.
Share linkHolds travel in the link (one parameter per role), so a friend sees the same Recipe.
  • The starting paint's role holds nothing else. It already is the paint you chose (no lock or pot there). To change it, choose a new starting paint.
  • Choosing a pot replaces a lock on the same role (the pot's colour becomes the target). Releasing the pot gives back the generated colour, not the old lock.
  • ⚓ Move here does not show on a held column. Release it first, so two of your choices never fight over one role (the pin study's rule).
  • No role reuses a held pot. Held pots are taken first, as the starting paint is today.
  • Release all. A text action under the palette, “Release 2 holds”, when any hold exists: the one way back to a fully generated scheme.

8. When a choice does not fit

From Solver and Onshape: say which choice causes the problem, and how much, in plain words. One line under the live palette, in the matching note's place, shown only when a held role is Poor (over ΔE 10 from its generated target):

Metal: Balthasar Gold is ΔE 22.9 from this scheme's metal (Poor). Keep it · Show closer pots
A chosen pot far from the scheme. “Keep it” dismisses the line for this hold; the ΔE stays on the card.
Accent: your locked orange has no pot within ΔE 10 under “Only my paints”. Show the closest to buy
A locked colour that the filter cannot match. This replaces a silent Poor tag.

Not proposed: moving the harmony to fit a held pot. A held colour does not steer the other roles (Realtime Colors does this; our harmonies come from the starting paint, which is clearer). If Ryan wants “fit the scheme to my pots”, it is a later step: Start from this pot (◎) already exists for one colour.

9. Verdict

Build direction A, in three steps.
  1. One model (a fix). A lock belongs to a role and reaches the Recipe. Columns that are not roles show as Extra, with a footnote. This fixes today's lock and needs no new control.
  2. Words. “Starting paint” and “Starting colour” replace “your paint” and “your colour”; the dock says “Move here” beside the ★ tag; the Shelf's menu says “Start a scheme from this”.
  3. Choose a pot (the pin). 📌 Choose pot… on each Recipe card (from the pin study, direction A), shown on the palette column too, with the fit line.

Later, if wanted: a per-role reroll (Eat This Much's shuffle) and a “similar ↔ bold” dial for Roll (Fontjoy, Huemint).
