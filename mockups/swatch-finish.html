Swatches that show the paint and its finish

The owner asked for a design pass on the swatches. The aim: each swatch must show the paint and the kind of paint. This study shows what we draw now, measures it, and gives three directions. It changes only how a swatch is drawn. The hex values themselves are the subject of a sibling study, docs/COLOUR_CHECK.md. All renders below use real paints and their real hex values from src/data/paints.json (dataset 1.6.0).

Page theme

0What we have now

The data has 3,225 paints in 10 types. A swatch gets an overlay from its type, or from its fx field. The function is fxCls in src/js/ui.js. A text row can also show a finish tag (finishTag). The Shelf cell shows the overlay only. It has no tag and no icon.

1Audit

Method. Playwright (Chromium) drew each 64px swatch on a light page. A script read every pixel inside the 1px edge. It then found two numbers with color.js (CIEDE2000, D65). Shift is the ΔE 2000 from the hex to the swatch’s mean colour (mean in linear light), with any finish icon or mark hidden, so it measures the overlay on the colour only. True area is the part of the whole swatch, icon or mark included, within ΔE 2.0 of the hex (“Excellent”, §3.2). §2 says an overlay must convey the finish and never alter the colour. A shift under 2 keeps the colour “Excellent”.

Findings

  1. The overlays shift the colour. Only 21–60% of a metal or wash swatch is within ΔE 2.0 of its hex. The mean colour moves too. The worst is the gloss overlay: Blood For The Blood God shifts by ΔE 10.4 (“Poor”). The white highlight on near-black washes and inks is next: Dark Tone 6.5, Burnt Umber 6.7 (“Loose”). Retributor Armour shifts 3.1, Agrellan Earth 3.2. Flat paints shift 0.1 (the 1px edge). §2 says the overlay must never alter the colour. On dark paints it does, because a 40% white highlight is a large step in L* near black.
  2. A wash looks like a satin flat. The wash overlay is a highlight top-left and a shadow bottom-right. That is the same idea as the metal sheen, only weaker. Nothing in it says “translucent”. Next to Dawnstone, Nuln Oil reads as a slightly shiny grey base paint.
  3. A contrast paint and a wash look almost the same. The two gradients differ by 5° and a few per cent of alpha. Both use the same drop icon in the tag. A painter cannot tell a Contrast pot from a Shade pot in the Shelf.
  4. Silver metals read as grey flats at 28px. At 64px the sheen works. At 28px the highlight is about 8px wide and soft. Leadbelcher (#969696) in the hero chip looks like a light grey base. Golds keep their read, because the hue helps.
  5. 22 of the 55 effect paints get no overlay. They have no fx, and effect is not in FX_BY_TYPE. Examples: Hexwraith Flame, Nihilakh Oxide, Stormshield, the Scale75 FX fluorescents. In the Shelf they are flat squares.
  6. Some finishes hide in other types. 5 Vallejo gloss paints (Gloss White, Glossy Black, Fuel Stains Gloss…) and 36 fluorescent paints are layer. 4 Citadel “-gloss” shades get the wash overlay, not gloss. This is a data question (an fx value), not a drawing question.
  7. dry needs nothing on the swatch. Dry paint dries matte, as a base does. 29 Citadel Dry paints share a hex with their Layer twin (COLOUR_CHECK §3). The name carries “(Dry)”, which is the right place for it.
  8. The tag colours break the single-meaning rule (§3.5). The metallic tag uses --warning-text. It looks like a “Fair” match tag. The contrast tag uses --accent, the selection colour. A finish is neither a warning nor a selection.
  9. The finish icon is not on the swatch. The tag icon is 10px and sits in text rows only. On the Shelf, in the hero chip, and in the live palette band, the overlay is the only finish signal.
  10. The edge fails on near-black paints in dark theme. The edge is 12% black, drawn on the paint. On a near-black paint it is black on black. On the dark page (#141110), 127 paints have a contrast under 1.2:1 with the page, and the edge does not help any of them (Abaddon Black: 1.12:1, edge 1.12:1). The swatch has no visible end. In light theme the edge works: 147 paints are close to the page, and all 147 get an edge of 1.2:1 or more. A light edge in dark theme (16% white) lifts Abaddon Black’s edge to 1.29:1 and fixes all 127.
  11. Hex values set limits that no drawing can fix. 33 paints are pure #000000 or #FFFFFF (COLOUR_CHECK §3.2). Washes use two conventions: Citadel shades are recorded thinned over white (Nuln Oil #9E9A98, a mid grey), while Army Painter Dark Tone is the liquid (#0C060E, near black). Both are “a black wash”. A swatch shows the hex it gets, so this needs the COLOUR_CHECK wash rule first.

2Three directions

Each direction is drawn on the same 14 real paints, in both themes, at all four sizes. The flat paints (top row) are the control: a direction must leave them as they are now.

A · Tune today’s overlays

Same idea, better numbers. One overlay per finish class, each with its own shape. Metal: a softer sheen plus one thin specular streak, which survives at 28px. Wash: a dark pooling at the edges, the way a wash collects in recesses, with no highlight. Contrast: a vertical light-to-dark fade (raised to recess), so it no longer matches the wash. Effects: smaller gloss and slime highlights. The Shelf cell gets the finish icon in its bottom-left corner. The swatch edge is a light line in dark theme. The tag becomes neutral, and contrast gets its own half-drop icon. Limit: on a near-black wash (Dark Tone) the pooling cannot get darker, so only the Shelf icon shows the finish.

B · One finish mark, in one corner

The colour field is the hex, every pixel. No gradient lies on the body. The finish goes into one small square in the bottom-right corner. The square is the swatch’s ink colour (textOn), with the finish icon cut in the paint colour. One icon per finish: gem (metal), drop (wash, shade, ink, glaze), half-drop (contrast), glint (gloss), blobs (slime), dots (texture), star (other effects). It works at every size and in forced colours, and it is the same in both themes.

C · A material swatch: show how the paint sits

The shape tells the finish. Metal: a bevelled plate, the hex in the centre. Wash, shade, ink, glaze: a brush stroke on white primer that thins to the right. Contrast: the hex on the raised part, a darker pooled band in the recess. Effects: today’s bespoke overlays. This is the most physical, but it changes the swatch shape that §3.5 sets (a square of colour), and the stroke means something different for each wash convention (finding 11).

Shift is ΔE 2000 (lower is truer). True is the per cent of the swatch within ΔE 2.0 of the hex (higher is truer). B’s true area is about 92%, not 100%: its mark covers the corner. The mark is not paint, so B’s body shift is the honest number for its colour. In C the translucent shift is large by design: the stroke shows the primer. The edge numbers are in finding 10.

3Recommendation

Do B for the finish, with A’s edge and tag fixes. B is the only direction that keeps every pixel of the colour field true (body shift 0.1 for every class), and it is the only one where a wash, a contrast paint and a metal read as three different things at 28px. It also gives the Shelf cell, the hero chip and the pot band the finish signal they lack today. Take three things from A as well: the 16% white edge in dark theme, a neutral finish tag, and the half-drop icon for contrast. These are small and fix measured faults.

The risk of B is that it looks like a UI badge, not paint. The mark is loud on dark paints (a white square on Dark Tone). If the owner wants the swatch to feel like paint, use A instead: its body shift is under 2 for every paint shown except gloss (2.8), and it costs half a day. Do not build C now. It is the most physical, but it moves the colour most for translucent media (shift 4.9–21), it changes the §3.5 square, and its brush stroke is wrong until the wash hex convention is settled (finding 11). C’s brush stroke can come back later as a single “over primer” view in the Recipe’s wash step.

Questions for the owner

  1. Is the body of a swatch allowed to carry any gradient?B says no: every pixel of the colour field is the hex. A says a small, measured one (shift ≤ 2) is fine.
  2. B’s mark or A’s icon on the Shelf?B’s square is always there and is easy to read. A’s icon is lighter and looks less like a badge, but it is thin on mid-tone paints.
  3. Which wash convention do we keep?Thinned over white (Citadel) or the liquid (Army Painter)? Direction C, and any future “over primer” view, needs one rule. COLOUR_CHECK asks a near question.
  4. Should gloss and fluorescent layers carry an fx?5 Vallejo gloss paints and 36 fluorescent paints are layer today, and 22 effect paints have no fx.

One icon per finish (the rule)

The icon comes from the paint’s finish: its fx first, then its type. Flat paints (base, layer, dry) get none. The glyphs follow the icon set (§3.5): a 20px grid, a 1.75px stroke, square ends, mitred joins, currentColor. At 12–13px the stroke is 2.25px. This replaces today’s filled 16px glyphs, where wash and contrast share the drop. The tag in a text row uses the same glyph. Contrast gets its own glyph: the drop with its lower half filled. It says “the colour pools low”, which is how a Contrast paint shades itself.

Two rows need a data change, not a drawing change: fx: "fluo" for the 36 fluorescent and neon layer paints, and fx: "gloss" for the 5 Vallejo gloss layer paints. Both are keyword seeds in build-dataset.mjs, with a SOURCES.md note. The 22 effect paints with no fx get the plain effect star. Shade, ink and glaze share the wash drop: they are one finish class (§5.2), and the tag word tells them apart.

Four ways to sit the icon on paint

The same 23 real paints in every variant, at the app’s real sizes: the Shelf cell (64px, some with the owned ✓ or to-buy cart badge top-right), the list row pot (40px), the hero chip (28px), and the live palette column (target block over a 26px pot band; here the target is the pot hex too). The flat paints (first three) are the control: they must show no icon. The hard cases are the near-blacks and near-whites: Abaddon Black, Dark Tone, Burnt Umber, Black Templar, Blood For The Blood God, White Scar, Titanium White, Stormhost Silver, Stormshield.

Measured: the icon against what it sits on

WCAG 1.4.11 asks 3:1 for a graphic that you need to understand the content. The finish is also in the name, the tag and the screen-reader label, so the icon is a second signal. We still hold it to 3:1. The numbers are WCAG contrast ratios from the hexes (the same formula as textOn). “All 747” is every non-flat paint in paints.json 1.6.0: 706 non-flat types, 36 fluorescent and neon layers, 5 gloss layers.

Washes: the liquid in the pot, or one coat over white?

The data mixes two conventions today. These are the four washes as paints.json has them, and an illustration of each in the other convention.

  • The liquid in the pot is the colour you see when you look into the open pot. Almost every wash is then near black or a very dark brown. Army Painter’s values are like this (Dark Tone L* 2, Strong Tone L* 10).
  • One coat over white primer (a drawdown) is the colour the wash leaves on a model. It shows the tint and how dark the wash goes. Citadel’s values are like this (Nuln Oil L* 64, Agrax Earthshade L* 65).
  • Matching by the pot colour puts a wash next to dark flats. Black and brown washes land close together, so the swatch says little and the finish icon does the work.
  • Matching by the effect compares what each wash does on the model. A black wash and a brown wash stay apart, and the swatch shows a colour you can judge.
  • Mixed, as now, matching fails across brands. Nuln Oil and Dark Tone are both black washes, but they are ΔE 49.7 apart. Agrax Earthshade and Strong Tone are ΔE 47.6 apart. Within one convention the same pairs are close: Nuln Oil to Agrax 6.8, Dark Tone to Strong Tone 8.6.

The illustrations are not measured values. Each one keeps the data’s Lab hue and chroma and moves only L* to the other brand’s level (L* 10 for the pot, L* 64 for one coat). A real drawdown of a brown wash is more saturated than this. Real values need a photo of a drawdown or of the liquid under one light (COLOUR_CHECK.md, method).

Recommendation

Build (a), the bare icon in the swatch’s ink. It is the lightest, which is what Ryan liked in A. It is the app’s own way to put a thing on paint: the copy button and the lock badge already draw in textOn, so (a) adds no new look. It passes 3:1 on every one of the 747 non-flat paints (lowest 4.28:1), in both themes, because it does not depend on the theme. It sits opposite the mark badge’s corner on the same right edge, so state (a filled badge, top) and kind (an outline icon, bottom) stay two languages (§3.5). (d) is the runner-up: it ties the swatch to the row tag, but the box adds weight at 28px and 40px. Drop (b): it reads as a second state badge and fails as a shape on dark paints. Drop (c): it does not read under 64px.

For the washes, pick one convention before any wash value changes. The pot colour is easy to sample but makes most washes near black. The drawdown is closer to what a painter needs from a match. If Ryan keeps “the liquid in the pot”, the finish icon becomes the main wash signal, and (a) carries it well.
