Shelf map — which tones am I missing?

A study for PLAN §5 item 61 (Ryan, 2026-11-06: "place all my paints on the wheel … the colour wheel is kinda a mini map in video games"). Five ways to draw the whole shelf as a map. Every dot and cell is a real paint from src/data/paints.json, placed by its Lab hue, chroma and lightness (§7). Tap a dot or a cell to see the paints there, or, for a gap, the three closest pots that fill it (true ΔE 2000, §2).

The problem in one line: a wheel shows two of a colour's three dimensions. Hue is the angle and strength (chroma) is the distance from the centre, but lightness is lost. A navy and a sky blue land on one spot. So each direction below makes a different trade with that third dimension.

AMinimap — one dot per paint

The plain answer to "where are my paints". The disc is the colour plane (Lab a*, b*): hue around, strength out. Each owned paint is a dot in its own colour; a paint to buy is a ring in --buy. A hue family (a 20° band, the Browse order's) with no paint at all is fogged, like an unexplored part of a game map. The greys have no hue, so they sit in their own strip, dark to light.

ownedto buyno paint in this family

A colour wheel with one dot for each paint. The table below the directions lists the same counts.

Judged

  • For: the most "minimap" of the five; you read clusters and holes at a glance; no new words to learn; a small version fits the Shelf's header or the Studio's wheel (direction E).
  • Against: no lightness. A family can look full while every paint in it is mid-tone, so it cannot answer "can I shade this green". 220 dots overlap near the centre (the muted browns and drabs). A dot is too small to tap (8px), so a tap takes the nearest paint.

BRing map — hue around, lightness in rings

The wheel cut into cells: 18 hue bands around, 5 lightness rings from shade (inside) to highlight (outside). A cell with paints is filled with their mean colour and its count; an empty cell is hatched, with the colour it is missing as a small dot. The centre is the greys, in the same 5 rings. Strength is dropped (it is the least useful of the three for a painter: a weak and a strong red both shade a red).

a cell with paints (its count)a gapinside → outside: shade → highlight

Judged

  • For: both questions in one round picture; it still reads as "the wheel"; the bins remove the clutter (90 cells for any shelf size); each cell is a focusable button.
  • Against: the inner rings are small (the shade ring is about 12px deep at 360px wide), so the darks — which a painter needs most — get the least room. A round chart is harder to compare than a grid: two cells of the same row are not side by side.

CTone grid — the wheel unrolled

The plan's second picture: the same 18 hue bands across, and the 5 lightness bands down, from highlight to shade. Each cell shows up to 8 of its pots as small swatches and its count. An empty cell is dashed and hatched, with the colour it is missing in its centre: "no dark green to shade with". The last column is the greys. On a phone (≤520px) the grid turns, so the hues run down and you scroll the page, never sideways.

a gap (dashed) a pot to buy

Judged

  • For: the most useful answer: every gap is a named cell ("Dark · Teal") with pots to fill it. Every cell is the same size, so the darks get as much room as the lights. It is a grid of real buttons: arrows, Tab and a screen reader work without a second view. It shows the real pots, not dots. A Tone Pro family would fill one column top to bottom (item 57).
  • Against: it does not look like a wheel, so the "minimap" feel is gone; the hue bands are cut at fixed lines, so a paint on a line sits in one cell only.

DFog of war — floors of the colour space

The most game-like. The disc is every colour at one lightness (a "floor"); pick a floor from shade to highlight, as a game map shows one level at a time. A colour within ΔE 2000 ≤ 8 of a paint you own (any lightness) is clear; the rest is fogged. The dots are your paints on this floor. Out-of-gamut colours (no paint can be that colour on a screen) are left blank, so each floor has its own real shape: the light floor has yellows, the dark floor has blues.

clear = within ΔE 8 of a paint you ownfogged = nothing close

Judged

  • For: it shows coverage as an area, not a count: one paint clears a patch round it, so "I have a red near that" counts. It is the most fun, and it is the Lab solid of item 63 cut in slices, so it leads to the 3D study.
  • Against: five pictures to read one shelf; the ΔE 8 radius is a choice we make for the painter; it is the slowest to draw (about 100k ΔE 2000 per floor, done once and cached); it needs the table for a keyboard and a screen reader.

EIn the Studio — "show my paints" on the scheme wheel

The plan's second place: direction A as a faint layer under the Studio's wheel, so a scheme's nodes sit beside the paints you own. Here: Mephiston Red, triadic. A node in a fogged family says "you own nothing near this" before you open the Recipe. A catch: the Studio's wheel is HSL; this map is Lab. The layer must draw in the wheel's own space (HSL hue and saturation), or a dot sits about 10–30° from where the node for the same colour is.

a scheme nodeyour paints, faint

Judged

  • For: it puts the shelf where the decision is made; no new view; one toggle.
  • Against: a busy wheel gets busier (nodes, badges, the neutral note, and now 200 dots); it answers "do I own this scheme" which the Recipe already answers paint by paint. Best as a later extra to A or C, not the first build.

The same counts as a table

The text path that A, B and D need (§3.5: never only a picture). Owned paints per hue band and lightness band, for the shelf and filter above; a 0 is a gap. In the app this is the Map view's "Show as a table".

Recommended: C, with A on top of it. The Shelf's Map view (beside Grid and List) opens on a small A minimap as its header, the overview ("where are my colours"), and the C grid under it, the work ("which tone do I buy"). A tap on a fogged family in A scrolls to that column in C. C is accessible by itself, so no second table is needed. B is the fallback if a round picture is a must. D and E stay as later ideas: D after the 3D study (item 63), E as a "show my paints" toggle on the Studio's wheel.
